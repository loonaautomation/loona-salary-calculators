How it is calculated

  • What comes off a French salary: employee social contributions (cotisations salariales) — health, old-age pension (retraite de base), complementary pension (Agirc-Arrco), unemployment insurance, CSG/CRDS and small levies such as the cadre APEC contribution. Together they take roughly 21–23% of gross for a non-cadre and around 25% for a cadre once the employer's chosen prévoyance and mutuelle (top-up health) schemes are included.
  • Why this page is an estimate: unlike most countries, France has no single statutory employee percentage. The total depends on the salary ceiling multiples (plafond de la Sécurité sociale, €48,060 in 2026), your convention collective and your employer's insurance contracts. Two people on the same gross in different companies can differ by 1–3 points.
  • Income tax (prélèvement à la source, PAS): since 2019 income tax is withheld monthly, but at a personalised rate set by the tax office from your household's last return — 0% for many lower earners, rising with income and family situation. It cannot be computed from salary alone, so this calculator shows net before PAS (the "net à payer avant impôt" line on a real payslip) and the tax row shows zero by design.
  • Employer charges: your employer pays roughly a further 42–45% on top of gross — none of it is deducted from your pay.

Worked example (estimate): non-cadre on €42,000.00 → contributions of about 23% = €9,660.00, net before income tax €32,340.00 a year. A cadre on the same gross keeps about €31,500.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert a French gross salary (brut) to net?

As a rule of thumb, multiply by about 0.77 for a non-cadre employee or 0.75 for a cadre to get the net before income tax. On €42,000 gross that is roughly €32,340 (non-cadre) or €31,500 (cadre) a year. This page applies those standard estimates — your payslip can differ by a point or two either way.

Why is this calculator labelled an estimate?

Because France does not set one employee contribution rate in law. The total is the sum of many scheme rates, some capped at the Social Security ceiling (€48,060 in 2026), plus prévoyance and mutuelle premiums chosen by your employer. The 23% / 25% figures are the standard planning estimates used across the French payroll industry.

What is the prélèvement à la source (PAS)?

Income tax withheld from your pay each month at a rate the tax office (DGFiP) calculates from your household's previous tax return and sends to your employer. It depends on total household income and family quotient, not just this salary — which is why no salary-only calculator can include it exactly. Your net after PAS is the bottom line of the payslip, the 'net à payer'.

What is the difference between cadre and non-cadre?

Historically cadres (executives/engineers) paid extra pension contributions; since the 2019 Agirc-Arrco merger the difference is small — mainly the APEC contribution (0.024% employee share) and typically pricier prévoyance cover. In practice cadres land near 25% total charges and non-cadres near 23%, the estimates used here.

How much is the minimum wage (SMIC) in France?

The full-time SMIC is roughly €22,000 gross a year (about €1,800 a month, indexed each January). At that level employee contributions follow the same percentages, and the PAS rate is usually 0%, so net before and after tax are nearly the same.

How it works — the formula

Estimated net (before income tax) = gross × (1 − charges). The charges term is the sum of all employee cotisations as a share of gross: about 0.23 for a non-cadre employee and about 0.25 for a cadre in the standard 2026 structure, blending the capped pension slices (charged only up to the €48,060 Social Security ceiling) with the uncapped CSG/CRDS and insurance lines. The result is the payslip's 'net à payer avant impôt sur le revenu'; your personal PAS withholding is then taken from it to reach the final 'net à payer'.

Worked example

Non-cadre, gross €42,000.00 a year (estimate). Employee contributions ≈ 23% × €42,000.00 = €9,660.00. Net before income tax = €32,340.00 a year — €2,695.00 a month. The same salary as a cadre: contributions ≈ 25% = €10,500, net ≈ €31,500 a year (€2,625 a month), still before any PAS withholding.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my real payslip show a slightly different percentage?

Because of the capped slices: pension contributions are charged in bands around the Social Security ceiling (€4,005 a month in 2026), so the blended rate shifts a little with salary level, and your mutuelle premium is a fixed euro amount, not a percentage. The estimate stays within about a point for typical salaries.

Are the 23% and 25% figures official?

There is no official single figure to publish — URSSAF publishes each scheme's rate separately. The totals used here are the standard sums of those rates for a typical private-sector contract, the same basis French payroll bureaus use for quick brut-net conversions.

Does the calculator include the CSG?

Yes — CSG/CRDS (about 9.7% of 98.25% of gross) is the largest single employee line and is inside the 23% / 25% totals.

What will my net be after income tax?

Take the figure here and apply your PAS rate, shown on your last tax notice (avis d'imposition) and on every payslip. A single person with no other income on €42,000 gross typically has a PAS rate in the mid single digits — but use your own rate, not a guess.

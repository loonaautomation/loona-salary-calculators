How it is calculated

VAT in Lithuania is charged at 21% on most goods and services. The two formulas are:

  • Add VAT: VAT = Net × 21 ÷ 100, and Gross = Net + VAT.
  • Remove VAT: Net = Gross ÷ (1 + 21 ÷ 100), and VAT = Gross − Net.

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard VAT rate in Lithuania?

The standard VAT rate in Lithuania is 21%. It applies to most goods and services. VAT is called PVM locally, amounts on this page are in euro (EUR), and the rates here were verified in November 2026 against European Commission — Your Europe: VAT rules and rates.

What are the reduced VAT rates in Lithuania and what do they apply to?

Lithuania applies reduced VAT at 12% for passenger transport, hotel accommodation, books and certain other supplies; 5% for medicines, books and newspapers and certain technical aids for disabled people. The rate that applies depends on the exact category of the goods or services, so confirm the category with the State Tax Inspectorate (VMI) if you are unsure.

How do I add VAT to a price in Lithuania?

Multiply the net price by 0.21 to get the VAT amount at the 21% standard rate, then add it to the net price — or use the Add VAT mode above. For example, on a net price of € 100.00 the VAT is € 21.00 and the gross price is € 121.00.

How do I remove VAT from a gross price in Lithuania?

Divide the gross price by 1.21 to get the net amount at the 21% standard rate, then subtract the net from the gross to get the VAT — or use the Remove VAT mode above. For example, a gross price of € 100.00 contains € 17.36 of VAT, leaving a net amount of € 82.64.

Which Lithuanian supplies get the 12% PVM rate?

The 12% reduced PVM rate covers supplies such as passenger transport, hotel accommodation and books, while 5% covers medicines, newspapers and certain technical aids. Everything else is taxed at Lithuania's 21% standard rate.

How it works — the formula

The calculator stores no data and does one piece of arithmetic per mode. In Add VAT mode it treats your amount as the net price: it multiplies the net by the chosen rate divided by 100 to get the VAT, then adds the two together for the gross price you charge. In Remove VAT mode it treats your amount as a gross, VAT-inclusive price — the kind printed on a Lithuania receipt or shelf label — and divides it by (1 + rate ÷ 100) to recover the net amount; the difference between the gross and the net is the VAT contained in the price. Because consumer prices in Lithuania are quoted gross and business prices are usually quoted net, the two modes are exact inverses: removing VAT from a gross figure and then adding it back returns the original amount, apart from rounding to two decimal places in EUR.

Frequently asked questions

What must a VAT invoice in Lithuania show?

A valid VAT invoice in Lithuania normally shows a unique invoice number, the invoice date, the seller's and buyer's names, addresses and VAT numbers, a description of the goods or services, the net amount, the VAT rate applied, the VAT amount and the gross total, all in EUR. If the reverse charge applies, the invoice states that and shows no VAT charged. Keep invoices for the retention period required by the State Tax Inspectorate (VMI).

I sell from Lithuania to customers in other countries — which VAT rate applies?

For business-to-business sales from Lithuania to another EU country, the supply is usually zero-rated in Lithuania and the customer accounts for VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism in their own country. Before zero-rating, validate the customer's VAT number free on the EU VIES service and keep the validation as evidence; distance sales of goods and digital services to consumers are normally taxed at the customer's country rate, often via the One Stop Shop (OSS).

Are consumer prices in Lithuania shown including VAT?

Yes. Prices offered to consumers in Lithuania must be displayed including VAT at the applicable rate (21% standard), so a shelf price is a gross price — use the Remove VAT mode to find the net amount inside it. Business-to-business prices are usually quoted net of VAT. Registration, returns and payment are handled by the State Tax Inspectorate (VMI).

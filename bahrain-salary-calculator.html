Bahrain Net Salary Calculator

Bahrain charges no income tax. The only payslip deduction is social insurance (SIO): 8% for Bahraini employees, and just 1% — the unemployment insurance contribution — for expatriates.

In short: Bahrain has no income tax. On a BHD 18,000.00 gross salary an expatriate pays only the 1% unemployment contribution (BHD 180.00) and nets BHD 17,820.00; a Bahraini employee pays 8% SIO social insurance. Bahrain is the one Gulf state where expatriates do have a small deduction.

How it is calculated

  • Income tax: Bahrain levies no personal income tax on salaries.
  • SIO — Bahraini employees: the employee contribution to the Social Insurance Organization is 8% of contributory salary (pension, disability and unemployment branches), on the Social Insurance Law's phased schedule; the employer pays a larger share on top.
  • SIO — expatriate employees: expatriates are outside the pension branches but are covered by unemployment insurance, so 1% of salary is deducted (the employer adds 3%). Bahrain is the exception to the Gulf rule that expatriates pay nothing.
  • End-of-service indemnity: expatriates accrue an indemnity under the Bahrain Labour Law — for the first three years, 15 days of salary per year in the standard monthly-paid case, rising with service — paid at the end of the job rather than deducted monthly. Recent reforms route expatriate indemnity through the SIO fund; use the Bahrain Indemnity Calculator linked below.

Worked example: a Bahraini citizen on BHD 18,000.00 gross pays 8% = BHD 1,440 and nets BHD 16,560 a year. An expatriate on the same salary pays only the 1% unemployment contribution — BHD 180.00 — and nets BHD 17,820.00.

Frequently asked questions

Is there income tax in Bahrain?

No. Bahrain has no personal income tax on employment income. The only statutory deduction from a salary is the SIO social insurance contribution described on this page.

Why do expatriates pay 1% in Bahrain but nothing in Qatar or Kuwait?

Because Bahrain's unemployment insurance branch covers expatriate employees, unlike the pension schemes of its neighbours. The expatriate employee pays 1% of salary toward it (employer 3%); the pension branches remain for Bahraini nationals only, at an 8% employee rate.

What does the Bahraini 8% SIO contribution cover?

It is the employee share for old-age pension, disability, death and unemployment cover under the Social Insurance Law, charged on the contributory salary. The employer pays a higher share on top of your salary; that part never reduces your net pay.

What end-of-service benefit do expatriates get in Bahrain?

An indemnity based on salary and years of service under the Labour Law — broadly 15 days per year for the first three years for monthly-paid staff, then a month per year, with resignation scales. It is a lump sum at the end of employment, not a monthly deduction: try our Bahrain Indemnity Calculator.

Are there plans for an income tax in Bahrain?

Bahrain has introduced a domestic minimum top-up tax for very large multinational groups (a corporate measure from 2025), but as of November 2026 there is no personal income tax and none has been legislated for salaries.

How it works — the formula

Net = gross − income tax − SIO contribution. The income tax term is zero in Bahrain. The SIO term is 1% of salary for an expatriate (unemployment branch only), so net = gross × 0.99; for a Bahraini employee it is 8%, so net = gross × 0.92. Employer SIO contributions are charged on top of salary and do not enter the formula.

Worked example

Expatriate, gross BHD 18,000.00 a year. Income tax: BHD 0. SIO unemployment contribution: 1% × BHD 18,000.00 = BHD 180.00. Net = BHD 17,820.00 a year — BHD 1,485.00 a month. A Bahraini citizen on the same salary pays 8% = BHD 1,440 and nets BHD 16,560 a year (BHD 1,380 a month).

Frequently asked questions

Is the 1% expatriate contribution really compulsory?

Yes. Unemployment insurance in Bahrain covers expatriate employees, and the 1% employee share is deducted from salary by law. In return, an expatriate who loses their job through no fault of their own can qualify for unemployment compensation under the scheme's conditions.

Does the citizen rate change over time?

The Bahraini employee rate has been rising on a legislated schedule and stands at 8% in 2026; the employer rate is higher and also scheduled upward. This page uses the 2026 employee figure.

Are my employer's SIO payments part of my net salary?

No. Employer contributions (18% for Bahraini staff in 2026, 4% for expatriates including the 3% unemployment share) are an additional cost on top of your gross salary.

How does Bahrain compare with the UAE for take-home pay?

In the UAE an expatriate also nets 100% of gross — there is no 1% deduction. On BHD 18,000 the Bahrain expatriate gives up BHD 180 a year to unemployment insurance, the price of the cover it buys.

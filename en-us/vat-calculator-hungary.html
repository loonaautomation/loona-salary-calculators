How it is calculated

VAT in Hungary is charged at 27% on most goods and services. The two formulas are:

  • Add VAT: VAT = Net × 27 ÷ 100, and Gross = Net + VAT.
  • Remove VAT: Net = Gross ÷ (1 + 27 ÷ 100), and VAT = Gross − Net.

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard VAT rate in Hungary?

The standard VAT rate in Hungary is 27%. It applies to most goods and services. VAT is called ÁFA locally, amounts on this page are in Hungarian forint (HUF), and the rates here were verified in November 2026 against European Commission — Your Europe: VAT rules and rates.

What are the reduced VAT rates in Hungary and what do they apply to?

Hungary applies reduced VAT at 18% for certain foodstuffs including some dairy and cereal products, and internet access; 5% for medicines, books, newspapers and certain basic foodstuffs such as fresh milk, eggs and poultry. The rate that applies depends on the exact category of the goods or services, so confirm the category with the National Tax and Customs Administration (NAV) if you are unsure.

How do I add VAT to a price in Hungary?

Multiply the net price by 0.27 to get the VAT amount at the 27% standard rate, then add it to the net price — or use the Add VAT mode above. For example, on a net price of Ft 100.00 the VAT is Ft 27.00 and the gross price is Ft 127.00.

How do I remove VAT from a gross price in Hungary?

Divide the gross price by 1.27 to get the net amount at the 27% standard rate, then subtract the net from the gross to get the VAT — or use the Remove VAT mode above. For example, a gross price of Ft 100.00 contains Ft 21.26 of VAT, leaving a net amount of Ft 78.74.

Why is Hungary's VAT rate the highest in Europe?

Hungary's 27% standard rate is the highest in the European Union and has applied since 2012. The government relies heavily on consumption taxes, while protecting essentials through 18% and 5% reduced rates. Because amounts are in forint (HUF) with no cents in everyday use, VAT amounts are normally rounded to whole forints.

How it works — the formula

The calculator stores no data and does one piece of arithmetic per mode. In Add VAT mode it treats your amount as the net price: it multiplies the net by the chosen rate divided by 100 to get the VAT, then adds the two together for the gross price you charge. In Remove VAT mode it treats your amount as a gross, VAT-inclusive price — the kind printed on a Hungary receipt or shelf label — and divides it by (1 + rate ÷ 100) to recover the net amount; the difference between the gross and the net is the VAT contained in the price. Because consumer prices in Hungary are quoted gross and business prices are usually quoted net, the two modes are exact inverses: removing VAT from a gross figure and then adding it back returns the original amount, apart from rounding to two decimal places in HUF.

Frequently asked questions

What must a VAT invoice in Hungary show?

A valid VAT invoice in Hungary normally shows a unique invoice number, the invoice date, the seller's and buyer's names, addresses and VAT numbers, a description of the goods or services, the net amount, the VAT rate applied, the VAT amount and the gross total, all in HUF. If the reverse charge applies, the invoice states that and shows no VAT charged. Keep invoices for the retention period required by the National Tax and Customs Administration (NAV).

I sell from Hungary to customers in other countries — which VAT rate applies?

For business-to-business sales from Hungary to another EU country, the supply is usually zero-rated in Hungary and the customer accounts for VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism in their own country. Before zero-rating, validate the customer's VAT number free on the EU VIES service and keep the validation as evidence; distance sales of goods and digital services to consumers are normally taxed at the customer's country rate, often via the One Stop Shop (OSS).

Are consumer prices in Hungary shown including VAT?

Yes. Prices offered to consumers in Hungary must be displayed including VAT at the applicable rate (27% standard), so a shelf price is a gross price — use the Remove VAT mode to find the net amount inside it. Business-to-business prices are usually quoted net of VAT. Registration, returns and payment are handled by the National Tax and Customs Administration (NAV).

How it is calculated

  • CPF — citizens & PRs: the employee contribution depends on age: 20% (55 or below), 18% (56–60), 12.5% (61–65), 7.5% (66–70), 5% (above 70) — rates from January 1, 2026. It is charged only on the first S$8,000 of monthly wages (the Ordinary Wage ceiling, at its final stepped level from January 1, 2026; the annual salary ceiling is S$102,000). Your employer adds 17% (age 55 or below) on top, never deducted from you. Third-year and later PRs pay full rates; new PRs pay graduated rates in their first two years.
  • CPF — foreign employees: no CPF at all, for either side. Take-home cash is correspondingly higher, but no CPF savings are built up.
  • Income tax: resident rates for Year of Assessment 2026 — 0% to S$20,000, then 2% / 3.5% / 7% / 11.5% / 15% / 18% / 19% / 19.5% / 20% / 22% / 23% up the bands to 24% above S$1,000,000. Your employee CPF is tax-deductible, and earned income relief (20% of earned income, capped at S$1,000) is applied. Unlike most countries, Singapore tax is not withheld monthly — IRAS bills you after assessment (most people pay by 12-month GIRO), so the monthly figure here spreads the annual tax evenly.

Worked example: on S$96,000, a citizen/PR under 55 pays CPF of 20% × S$8,000 × 12 = S$19,200; chargeable income is S$75,800 after CPF relief and earned income relief; tax S$3,056; take-home S$73,744. The live example above uses S$72,000.00: CPF S$14,400.00, tax S$1,712.00, take-home S$55,888.00.

Frequently asked questions

How much CPF do I pay in 2026?

If you are a citizen or PR aged 55 or below: 20% of your wages, but only on the first S$8,000 a month (the Ordinary Wage ceiling from January 1, 2026) — a maximum of S$1,600 a month, S$19,200 a year, for the employee share. The rate falls with age: 18% at 56–60, 12.5% at 61–65, 7.5% at 66–70 and 5% above 70.

Do foreigners pay CPF in Singapore?

No. CPF applies only to Singapore citizens and permanent residents. Foreign employees on work passes have no CPF deducted (and their employer pays none either), so their cash take-home is higher — but they receive no CPF retirement, housing or healthcare savings.

Is Singapore income tax deducted from monthly salary?

No — Singapore has no PAYE withholding. Your employer pays your full salary (minus CPF if applicable), and IRAS assesses income tax after the year ends. Most residents pay via interest-free GIRO installments over up to 12 months, which is why this calculator shows the tax as an even monthly equivalent.

What is the income tax rate in Singapore?

Residents pay progressive rates from 0% on the first S$20,000 to 24% above S$1,000,000 (YA 2026). A resident on S$96,000 with full CPF relief pays about S$3,056 — an effective rate near 3%. Non-residents (under 183 days) are generally taxed at a flat 24% on employment income instead.

Why is my CPF less than 20% of my salary?

Because of the wage ceiling: CPF is charged only on the first S$8,000 of monthly ordinary wages (and total contributions respect the S$102,000 annual salary ceiling). On a S$12,000 monthly salary the employee contribution is still S$1,600 — 13.3% of pay, not 20%.

How it works — the formula

Take-home = gross − CPF − income tax. CPF (citizens/PRs only) = age-band rate × min(monthly wage, S$8,000) × 12; it is zero for foreign employees. Income tax is charged on chargeable income = gross − employee CPF − earned income relief (20% of income, capped at S$1,000), on the resident scale: 0% to S$20,000; 2% to S$30,000; 3.5% to S$40,000; 7% to S$80,000; 11.5% to S$120,000; 15% to S$160,000; 18% to S$200,000; 19% to S$240,000; 19.5% to S$280,000; 20% to S$320,000; 22% to S$500,000; 23% to S$1,000,000; 24% above.

Worked example

Citizen/PR aged 55 or below, gross S$72,000.00 a year. Monthly wage is S$72,000.00 ÷ 12; CPF at 20% on wages up to the S$8,000 ceiling comes to S$14,400.00 a year. Chargeable income after CPF relief and the S$1,000 earned income relief gives income tax of S$1,712.00. Take-home = S$55,888.00 a year — S$4,657.33 a month in cash terms (remember the tax part is actually billed by IRAS after assessment, not withheld monthly).

Frequently asked questions

What does my employer pay into CPF?

At age 55 or below, 17% of wages (same S$8,000 ceiling) on top of your salary — so total CPF is 37% of wages up to the ceiling. It is a real part of your compensation even though it never touches your bank account until you use it for housing, or retirement.

I just became a PR — is my CPF really 20%?

Not immediately. New PRs pay graduated (lower) CPF rates in their first two years of PR status unless they elect full rates. This calculator uses the full rates that apply from the third year.

Are bonuses subject to CPF?

Yes for citizens/PRs — bonuses are 'additional wages' and attract CPF, subject to the S$102,000 annual salary ceiling across all wages. This calculator treats your whole entry as ordinary wages, which matches when the ceiling is not reached.

How does Singapore compare with Hong Kong or the Gulf for take-home?

Singapore sits between them: unlike the Gulf there is real income tax, but effective rates are low (about 3% at S$96,000 for a resident with CPF relief) — the bigger 'deduction' is CPF, which is your own savings rather than a tax.

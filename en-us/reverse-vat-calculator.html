How it is calculated

The formula is: net = gross ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100) and VAT = gross − net. Dividing is essential: VAT was added on top of the net amount, so the VAT inside a gross price is always less than the headline percentage of that gross price.

Frequently asked questions

What is a reverse VAT calculation?

It is the calculation that splits a VAT-inclusive (gross) price into its two parts: the net amount before VAT and the VAT amount itself. Businesses use it to reclaim VAT, book expenses correctly and check invoices where only the gross total is shown.

What is the formula to remove VAT?

Net = gross ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100), then VAT = gross − net. For a 24% rate you divide by 1.24; for 19% you divide by 1.19. Taking the rate percentage straight off the gross gives the wrong answer, because the VAT was calculated on the smaller net amount.

How does the €124 example work?

At Estonia's 24% standard rate, a gross amount of €124 divides by 1.24 to give exactly €100 net, so the VAT inside the price is €124 − €100 = €24. The worked example on this page is calculated live by the tool itself.

When would I need to reverse-calculate VAT?

When a receipt or invoice shows only the total, when you are reclaiming VAT on business expenses, when you compare prices quoted with and without VAT, or when you need the net figure for your bookkeeping or a VAT return.

Is this the same as the reverse charge mechanism?

No. The reverse charge is an EU accounting rule that shifts who reports VAT on cross-border B2B supplies. This reverse VAT calculator is simply the arithmetic that removes VAT from a gross amount — the two share a name but are different things.

How it works — the formula

A gross price is the net price multiplied by (1 + rate ÷ 100). Reversing that multiplication is a division: net = gross ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100). At 24% the divisor is 1.24, at 20% it is 1.20, at 8.1% (Switzerland) it is 1.081. The VAT amount is then simply what is left: gross − net. This is why the VAT inside a €120 price at 20% is €20 and not €24 — 20% was charged on the €100 net, not on the €120 total. Frequently asked questions

How is rounding handled when I remove VAT?

The calculator divides exactly and rounds only for display, to two decimals. On real invoices, rounding is done per line or per invoice according to national rules, so a hand-checked total can differ by a cent — that is normal and accepted by tax authorities.

Can I reverse-calculate a reduced VAT rate?

This page uses each country's standard rate, which covers most goods and services. If your item was taxed at a reduced rate (food, books, medicines and similar categories), the split will differ — check the reduced rates listed on that country's VAT page and confirm which rate was actually charged on your invoice.

Does removing VAT tell me how much I can reclaim?

It tells you the VAT contained in the price, which is the starting point. Whether you can actually reclaim it depends on your VAT registration, the type of expense and your country's rules — entertainment and private use are commonly excluded.

Malaysia Net Salary Calculator

Gross to net in Malaysia: EPF at 11% of wages, SOCSO and EIS on wages up to the RM6,000 monthly ceiling, and income tax (collected monthly as PCB) on the resident scale after the standard reliefs.

In short: on a RM 72,000.00 gross salary you net about RM 61,086.00 a year (RM 5,090.50 a month): RM 7,920.00 of EPF, a small SOCSO + EIS amount, and RM 2,490.00 of income tax (PCB). Most employees with extra reliefs (lifestyle, insurance, children) pay less tax than shown — this page applies only the universal reliefs.

How it is calculated

  • EPF (KWSP): the employee contributes 11% of wages with no wage ceiling (the employer adds 13% up to RM5,000 a month, 12% above). It is your retirement savings, not a tax.
  • SOCSO (PERKESO) + EIS: social security and employment insurance on wages up to the RM6,000 monthly ceiling. The statutory amounts come from contribution tables; this calculator uses the standard approximation — about 0.5% for SOCSO and 0.2% for EIS on insured wages (RM72,000 a year at most), which matches the table values closely at typical salaries.
  • Income tax / PCB: resident scale — 0% to RM5,000; 1% to RM20,000; 3% to RM35,000; 6% to RM50,000; 11% to RM70,000; 19% to RM100,000; 25% to RM400,000; 26% to RM600,000; 28% to RM2,000,000; 30% above — applied to chargeable income after the RM9,000 personal relief and EPF relief (capped at RM4,000). A RM400 rebate applies if chargeable income is RM35,000 or less. Employers withhold it monthly as PCB (Potongan Cukai Bulanan); the annual return settles any difference.
  • Not included: optional reliefs that cut the tax further — lifestyle (RM2,500), life insurance, medical, children, SSPN and others — plus EPF voluntary top-ups and zakat rebates.

Worked example: on RM120,000 — EPF RM13,200; SOCSO RM360 and EIS RM144 (ceiling applies); chargeable income RM107,000; tax RM11,150; net RM95,146 a year. The live example above uses RM 72,000.00: EPF RM 7,920.00, tax RM 2,490.00, net RM 61,086.00.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage is EPF in Malaysia?

The employee rate is 11% of wages (you may elect to contribute more). The employer adds 13% if you earn RM5,000 a month or less, and 12% above that. There is no wage ceiling on EPF, unlike SOCSO and EIS.

What is PCB?

Potongan Cukai Bulanan — the monthly tax deduction your employer withholds and pays to LHDN (the Inland Revenue Board) as an advance on your annual income tax. When you file your return (BE form), the PCB you have paid is credited against the assessed tax; overpayments are refunded.

How much are SOCSO and EIS?

Both are charged on wages up to a RM6,000 monthly ceiling using PERKESO contribution tables. In percentage terms the employee pays roughly 0.5% (SOCSO, first category) and 0.2% (EIS) of insured wages — at the ceiling, about RM30 and RM12 a month respectively. This calculator applies those percentages to wages up to RM72,000 a year.

How much tax do I pay on RM100,000 in Malaysia?

With only the standard reliefs: EPF RM11,000, chargeable income RM87,000 after the RM9,000 personal relief and RM4,000 EPF relief cap, and tax of about RM7,620 a year — plus SOCSO/EIS of about RM504. Net is roughly RM80,876 before any extra reliefs. Try RM100,000 above to see it live.

Do foreigners pay the same tax in Malaysia?

Tax residents (in Malaysia 182+ days) use the same scale and reliefs. Non-residents pay a flat 30% with no reliefs, and their EPF is optional rather than compulsory. This calculator models a tax resident.

How it works — the formula

Net = gross − EPF − SOCSO − EIS − income tax. EPF = 11% of gross. SOCSO ≈ 0.5% and EIS = 0.2% of insured wages, with wages capped at RM72,000 a year (RM6,000 a month). Income tax is charged on chargeable income = gross − RM9,000 personal relief − EPF relief (the lower of your EPF and RM4,000), on the resident scale (0% to RM5,000; 1% to RM20,000; 3% to RM35,000; 6% to RM50,000; 11% to RM70,000; 19% to RM100,000; 25% to RM400,000; 26% to RM600,000; 28% to RM2,000,000; 30% above), less a RM400 rebate when chargeable income is RM35,000 or less. PCB spreads that annual tax over 12 months.

Worked example

Gross RM 72,000.00 a year, resident. EPF = 11% × RM 72,000.00 = RM 7,920.00. SOCSO + EIS on wages up to the RM72,000 ceiling come to RM 504.00 a year. Chargeable income = RM 72,000.00 − RM9,000 − RM4,000 (EPF relief cap), and tax on the resident scale is RM 2,490.00. Net = RM 61,086.00 a year — RM 5,090.50 a month.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my PCB different from this annual figure ÷ 12?

PCB is computed month by month on cumulative pay with the reliefs your employer knows about, and bonuses or arrears in a month can spike it. The annual assessment reconciles everything — this page shows the clean annual position.

Can I reduce my EPF below 11%?

The statutory employee rate is 11%; employees aged 60 and above pay a reduced rate, and you can always contribute more voluntarily. You cannot elect less than the statutory rate while it applies to you.

Does EPF count as a tax?

No — it is your own retirement savings, withdrawable under KWSP rules (and partly at age 50/55). It also earns annual dividends and gives up to RM4,000 of tax relief, which this calculator applies.

What if I earn under RM5,000 a month in Malaysia?

Below RM60,000 a year your tax is small: on RM48,000, chargeable income is about RM35,000 after reliefs, tax is a few hundred ringgit before the RM400 rebate — and EPF (RM5,280) is by far the biggest deduction, most of which is your own savings.

How it is calculated

  • Section 154A rate: 0.25% of the gross remittance if registered with and certified by PSEB; 1% if not (the 1% rate also covers export of other, non-IT services). The bank deducts it when the foreign currency is realised, and it is a final tax if you file your return and any required withholding statement.
  • Non-filer doubling: practitioners apply the Tenth Schedule so that persons not on the Active Taxpayer List pay 0.50% / 2% instead. This doubling is practitioner-sourced, not quoted from the section itself — confirm it in FBR's current withholding rate card before relying on it.
  • Validity: the Finance Act 2026 extended the 0.25% rate up to Tax Year 2029 (FBR Circular No. 02 of 2026-27).
  • Conditions to keep: proceeds through approved banking channels (an 80%-of-export-earnings banking-channel condition is widely reported by practitioners), an NTN and filed returns to stay on the Active Taxpayer List, and PSEB registration kept active.
  • Comparison column: the normal non-salaried slab table (First Schedule, Division I) — 0% to Rs 600,000; 15% to Rs 1.2M; Rs 90,000 + 20% to Rs 1.6M; Rs 170,000 + 30% to Rs 3.2M; Rs 650,000 + 40% to Rs 5.6M; Rs 1,610,000 + 45% above — plus the 10% section 4AB surcharge above Rs 10 million. The table was unchanged by the Finance Act 2026. The comparison treats the full remittance as taxable income with no expense deductions, so it flatters neither side deliberately — it shows the scale of the difference.

Worked example: Rs 10,000,000 of IT exports, PSEB-registered filer → 154A tax = Rs 25,000. Unregistered, the same income pays Rs 100,000. Under the normal slabs it would pay Rs 3,590,000.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax do freelancers pay in Pakistan?

IT-export income under Section 154A: 0.25% (PSEB-registered) or 1% of the gross remittance, final. Other freelance income falls under the normal non-salaried slabs.

What does Section 154A cover?

Export proceeds of computer software, IT services and IT-enabled services received through banking channels. The 0.25% rate runs to Tax Year 2029.

What if I am a non-filer?

Practitioners apply doubled rates (0.50% / 2%) under the Tenth Schedule. That doubling is practitioner-sourced — verify it in FBR's current rate card.

Is PSEB registration worth it?

At Rs 10M of exports it saves Rs 75,000 a year in tax alone (Rs 100,000 → Rs 25,000). For most active exporters, yes.

My client is in Pakistan — does 154A apply?

No. Local-client income is taxed under the normal non-salaried slabs, shown in the comparison line above.

How it works — the formula

Section 154A charges a final tax on the gross amount of each export remittance — not on profit: 0.25% if you are registered with and certified by the Pakistan Software Export Board (PSEB), and 1% if you are not. The bank deducts it when the foreign currency is realised. If you are not on the Active Taxpayer List, this calculator applies the practitioner-used Tenth Schedule doubling — 0.50% or 2%. Alongside, it runs the same income through the normal non-salaried slab table (First Schedule, Division I, plus the 10% section 4AB surcharge on tax where income exceeds Rs 10 million) so you can see the size of the difference; the comparison treats the whole remittance as taxable income with no expenses deducted.

Formula: 154A tax = gross annual remittances × your rate (0.25% / 0.50% / 1% / 2%)

Worked example

Annual IT-export remittances of Rs 6,000,000, PSEB-registered and a filer. Section 154A tax: 6,000,000 × 0.25% = Rs 15,000, leaving Rs 5,985,000. The same income under the normal non-salaried slabs would pay Rs 1,790,000 — so the regime saves Rs 1,775,000 here. Drop the PSEB registration and the 154A bill quadruples to Rs 60,000; keep the registration but stay a non-filer, and it doubles to Rs 30,000.

Frequently asked questions

Is the tax charged on each payment or on the yearly total?

On each remittance, at the moment the bank converts your foreign currency. The rate is a flat percentage, so the yearly total is simply the sum of the per-payment deductions — and nothing extra is charged on the same money at return-filing time while the tax stays final.

Do I still have to file a tax return if the tax is "final"?

Yes. The 154A deduction is final only when you file your return (and any required withholding statement). Filing is also what keeps you on the Active Taxpayer List and away from the non-filer rates — skipping the return puts the final-tax treatment itself at risk.

My foreign clients pay a local agent who pays me in rupees — does 154A apply?

The regime is built around export proceeds received in Pakistan through approved banking channels. Money that never arrives through the banking channel as an export remittance does not get the 0.25% / 1% treatment and falls under the normal rules instead — which is why the comparison column matters.

How long is the 0.25% PSEB rate guaranteed for?

The Finance Act 2026 extended it up to Tax Year 2029, ending 30 June 2029 (FBR Circular No. 02 of 2026-27). Beyond that it depends on future Finance Acts; this page is updated when the law changes.

Can I claim expenses against 154A income?

There is nothing to claim against: the tax is a percentage of the gross remittance, so expenses are irrelevant to the 154A figure. They matter only in the normal-slab comparison — which is why this calculator's comparison, with no expenses deducted, is the worst case for the normal route.

India Net Salary Calculator

Gross to in-hand in India for FY 2026–27 under the new regime (the default regime): the revised slabs, the ₹75,000 standard deduction, the Section 87A rebate that takes tax to zero up to ₹12 lakh of taxable income, 4% cess — and employee EPF on the standard assumption stated below.

In short: on a ₹1,500,000.00 gross salary (CTC-style pay) you take home about ₹1,380,900.00 a year (₹115,075.00 a month) in FY 2026–27: ₹97,500.00 of income tax including cess and ₹21,600.00 of employee EPF. Earn ₹12,75,000 or less and the income tax is zero thanks to the standard deduction plus the 87A rebate.

How it is calculated

  • Regime: the new regime (Section 115BAC), the default for FY 2026–27. The old regime — with HRA, 80C and other deductions — is not modelled; the new regime wins for most salaried staff without large deductions.
  • Standard deduction: ₹75,000 off your salary before the slabs.
  • Slabs (taxable income): 0% to ₹4,00,000; 5% to ₹8,00,000; 10% to ₹12,00,000; 15% to ₹16,00,000; 20% to ₹20,00,000; 25% to ₹24,00,000; 30% above. (These are the Budget 2025 slabs, unchanged by Budget 2026.)
  • Section 87A rebate: if taxable income is ₹12,00,000 or less, the tax is rebated by up to ₹60,000 — which wipes it out completely. Combined with the standard deduction, a salary up to ₹12,75,000 pays zero income tax. Just above ₹12 lakh, marginal relief caps the tax at the amount by which income exceeds ₹12 lakh, so there is no cliff.
  • Surcharge & cess: 10% surcharge above ₹50 lakh of taxable income, 15% above ₹1 crore and 25% above ₹2 crore (the new-regime cap), then 4% health & education cess on the total. Marginal relief on surcharge is not modelled.
  • EPF: the employee's 12% provident fund contribution, shown on the common assumption that basic pay is 50% of gross and the statutory wage ceiling of ₹15,000 a month applies (so EPF is capped at ₹1,800 a month = ₹21,600 a year). If your employer contributes 12% of full basic, your EPF — and the difference — will be larger. Professional tax (a small state levy, typically ₹0–₹200 a month) is excluded.

Worked example: on ₹18,00,000 — taxable income ₹17,25,000; slab tax ₹1,45,000; cess 4% → ₹1,50,800; EPF ₹21,600; in-hand ₹16,27,600 a year. The live example above uses ₹1,500,000.00: tax ₹97,500.00, EPF ₹21,600.00, in-hand ₹1,380,900.00.

Frequently asked questions

What are the new-regime tax slabs for FY 2026–27?

Nil up to ₹4,00,000 of taxable income; 5% from ₹4–8 lakh; 10% from ₹8–12 lakh; 15% from ₹12–16 lakh; 20% from ₹16–20 lakh; 25% from ₹20–24 lakh; and 30% above ₹24 lakh. Salary first gets the ₹75,000 standard deduction, and the Section 87A rebate then wipes out tax entirely up to ₹12,00,000 of taxable income.

How is a ₹12,75,000 salary completely tax-free?

Standard deduction ₹75,000 brings taxable income to exactly ₹12,00,000. The slab tax on ₹12 lakh is ₹60,000 — and the Section 87A rebate covers tax up to ₹60,000 when taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less. Tax after rebate: zero (and no cess, because cess applies to the post-rebate tax). Only EPF is deducted, if applicable.

What is marginal relief near ₹12 lakh?

Just above ₹12 lakh of taxable income, your tax is capped at the excess over ₹12 lakh. On ₹12,50,000 of taxable income the slab tax would be ₹67,500, but marginal relief limits it to ₹50,000 — you never lose more than the extra income you earned. The crossover is around ₹12,70,588 of taxable income.

Should I choose the old regime instead?

Only if your deductions are large: the old regime can win with big HRA exemptions, home-loan interest and 80C/80D claims — as a rough guide you need deductions well above ₹5–6 lakh at higher incomes before it competes. This calculator implements the new (default) regime; compare both if your deductions are substantial.

Why is my EPF shown as ₹21,600 a year?

Because of the assumption stated on the page: EPF at 12% of basic pay, with basic at 50% of gross and the statutory wage ceiling of ₹15,000 a month applied — 12% × ₹15,000 × 12 = ₹21,600. Many employers instead deduct 12% of the full basic, which would be much higher; check your payslip's PF wage to see which applies to you.

How it works — the formula

Taxable income = gross − ₹75,000 standard deduction. Tax is charged on the slab scale (0% to ₹4L; 5% to ₹8L; 10% to ₹12L; 15% to ₹16L; 20% to ₹20L; 25% to ₹24L; 30% above). If taxable income ≤ ₹12,00,000, subtract the Section 87A rebate (tax minus up to ₹60,000 — i.e. zero in practice). If it is just above ₹12 lakh, tax is capped at (taxable income − ₹12,00,000) by marginal relief. Add surcharge (10%/15%/25% by income level) and 4% cess. In-hand = gross − tax − EPF, where EPF = 12% × min(50% of gross, ₹1,80,000) on the stated assumption.

Worked example

Gross ₹1,500,000.00 a year. Taxable income = ₹1,500,000.00 − ₹75,000. Slab tax on that income, after the 87A rebate or marginal relief as applicable and with 4% cess, is ₹97,500.00. EPF on the stated assumption is ₹21,600.00. In-hand = ₹1,380,900.00 a year — ₹115,075.00 a month. Effective total deduction rate: ₹7.94%.

Frequently asked questions

Is gratuity or bonus taxed differently?

Bonuses are taxed as ordinary salary in the year received. Gratuity is exempt up to ₹20 lakh for most private-sector employees on retirement or exit, so it is not part of this monthly-pay calculation.

What about TDS — is it the same as this tax?

TDS is just this tax collected monthly by your employer based on your declared regime and investments. If too much is deducted, the excess returns as a refund when you file your ITR; too little and you pay the balance (possibly with advance-tax interest).

Does the calculator include the employer PF contribution?

No. Only the employee's 12% comes out of your pay. Many CTC offers also count the employer's 12% inside the CTC figure — if yours does, enter your gross excluding employer PF for a like-for-like result.

Why no HRA or 80C fields?

Because those belong to the old regime. Under the new regime the only standard reliefs are the ₹75,000 standard deduction and the 87A rebate, both built in here. If you rely on large HRA or deductions, run an old-regime comparison before filing.

Ireland Net Salary Calculator

Gross to net in Ireland for 2026, for a single employee with the standard credits: PAYE income tax at 20% up to €44,000 and 40% above, minus €4,000 of tax credits, plus the Universal Social Charge and PRSI at 4.2%.

In short: on a €50,000.00 gross salary you take home about €39,667.18 a year (€3,305.60 a month) in 2026: €7,200.00 of PAYE income tax (after €4,000 of credits), €1,032.82 of USC and €2,100.00 of PRSI. Married couples and extra credits change the figures — the basis here is a single person with standard credits.

How it is calculated

  • PAYE income tax: 20% on the first €44,000 (single person's standard rate cut-off for 2026) and 40% above — then subtract your tax credits: the €2,000 Personal Tax Credit plus the €2,000 Employee (PAYE) Tax Credit = €4,000 off the tax bill.
  • USC (Universal Social Charge): 0.5% on the first €12,012; 2% to €28,700; 3% to €70,044; 8% above. If your total income is €13,000 or less you are exempt from USC entirely.
  • PRSI: Class A employee rate 4.2% for 2026, charged once weekly pay passes €352 (about €18,304 a year); a tapered PRSI credit softens the step at the threshold in real payroll and is not modelled. The rate rises to 4.35% from 1 October 2026 on the published roadmap — this calculator uses 4.2% for the year.
  • Basis: single person, standard credits, no pension contributions or benefit-in-kind. A married one-earner couple gets a €53,000 cut-off; rent credit, medical insurance relief and other credits would raise the net.

Worked example: on €50,000.00 — PAYE scale tax €11,200 minus €4,000 credits = €7,200.00; USC €1,032.82; PRSI €2,100.00; net €39,667.18 a year.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax do I pay on €50,000 in Ireland in 2026?

On the single-person standard basis: PAYE of €7,200 (scale tax €11,200 minus €4,000 credits), USC of about €1,033 and PRSI of €2,100 — total deductions of about €10,333, leaving roughly €39,667 a year, or €3,306 a month.

What are the Irish tax credits for 2026?

The main ones for an employee are the Personal Tax Credit (€2,000) and the Employee (PAYE) Tax Credit (€2,000) — €4,000 combined, applied automatically through your Tax Credit Certificate. Budget 2026 left bands and credits unchanged from 2025.

What is USC and who is exempt?

The Universal Social Charge is a separate levy on gross income at 0.5% / 2% / 3% / 8% bands. If your total income is €13,000 or less you pay no USC at all; medical-card holders and some others get capped rates. Otherwise it applies from the first euro, with no personal credit against it.

What PRSI rate applies in 2026?

Class A employees pay 4.2% of gross pay above the €352 weekly threshold for most of 2026, rising to 4.35% from 1 October 2026 under the PRSI roadmap. Your employer pays 11.4% (higher rate) on top. This calculator applies 4.2% across the year.

I am married — how do the numbers change?

A married couple or civil partners with one income gets a standard rate cut-off of €53,000 (instead of €44,000) and double personal credits (€4,000 personal + €2,000 employee), so tax falls noticeably. This page models the single basis; add roughly the value of the wider band and extra credits to estimate the couple position.

How it works — the formula

Net = gross − PAYE − USC − PRSI. PAYE = (20% × first €44,000 + 40% × the rest) − €4,000 credits, floored at zero. USC is banded: 0.5% to €12,012, 2% to €28,700, 3% to €70,044, 8% above — and zero in total if income is €13,000 or less. PRSI = 4.2% of gross once annual pay passes about €18,304 (the €352 weekly threshold), zero below it.

Worked example

Gross €50,000.00 a year, single, 2026. PAYE: 20% × €44,000 + 40% × €6,000 = €11,200, minus €4,000 credits = €7,200.00. USC: €60.06 + €333.76 + the 3% band on the rest = €1,032.82. PRSI: 4.2% × €50,000.00 = €2,100.00. Net = €39,667.18 a year — €3,305.60 a month.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my payslip show different credits?

Your Tax Credit Certificate (from Revenue's myAccount) sets your personal cut-off and credits, including any flat-rate expenses, rent credit or other claims. This page uses the standard single-person position — check your certificate for your exact figures.

What is emergency tax?

If your employer has no Revenue Payroll Notification for you, pay is taxed on an emergency basis — progressively harsher week by week — until your details arrive. It is refunded through payroll once your RPN is in place.

Does Ireland tax bonuses differently?

No — bonuses are ordinary pay for PAYE, USC and PRSI in the month paid. Because PAYE is cumulative, a big bonus month usually evens out over the year.

How does the October 2026 PRSI rise affect me?

The employee rate goes from 4.2% to 4.35% on 1 October 2026, so the last quarter of 2026 costs an extra 0.15% of pay (about €19 on a €50,000 salary for the quarter). This calculator keeps 4.2% for the whole year, as labelled.

How it is calculated

To add VAT: VAT = net × rate ÷ 100, and gross = net + VAT. To remove VAT: net = gross ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100), and VAT = gross − net. Never calculate the VAT inside a gross price as a straight percentage of the gross — at 19% the VAT inside €119 is €19, not €22.61.

Frequently asked questions

What is VAT and how is it calculated?

VAT (value added tax) is a consumption tax charged as a percentage of the net price. VAT amount = net amount × rate ÷ 100, and the gross (VAT-inclusive) price = net + VAT. This calculator does that maths for the standard rate of 30 European countries.

How do I add VAT to a price?

Choose the country, enter the net amount, and select Add VAT. For example, a €100 net price in Germany at 19% gives €19 VAT and a €119 gross price. The result table shows net, VAT and gross side by side.

How do I remove VAT from a VAT-inclusive price?

Select Remove VAT (or use the dedicated Reverse VAT Calculator). The calculator divides the gross amount by 1 + rate ÷ 100: a €119 gross price in Germany at 19% is €100 net plus €19 VAT — you cannot find the VAT by simply taking 19% of the gross.

Which rate should I use — standard or reduced?

Most everyday goods and services use the standard rate shown in the country selector. Reduced rates apply only to specific categories such as food, books, medicines or passenger transport, and they differ by country — the rates table on this page lists each country's reduced rates. If in doubt, check the item on the dedicated country page.

Are the rates on this page up to date?

Yes. Every standard rate was verified in November 2026 against official sources — the European Commission, GOV.UK, the Swiss Federal Tax Administration and Skatteetaten. That includes the recent changes: Estonia is now 24% (since 1 July 2025), Finland 25.5%, Romania 21% and Slovakia 23%.

Frequently asked questions

Why do VAT rates differ so much across Europe?

Each country sets its own rates within EU minimum rules (a standard rate of at least 15%). That is why Luxembourg charges 17% and Hungary 27%, with most countries between 19% and 25%. Switzerland (8.1%) and the United Kingdom (20%) set their rates outside the EU system entirely.

Does VAT apply when I sell to another EU country?

Usually VAT is charged in the customer's country for cross-border B2B and many B2C services, often through the reverse-charge mechanism or the EU One Stop Shop (OSS). This calculator works out amounts at a chosen country's rate; which country's rate legally applies depends on the type of supply, so take advice for regular cross-border trade.

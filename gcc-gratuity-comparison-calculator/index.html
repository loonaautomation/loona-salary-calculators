GCC Gratuity Comparison Calculator

One salary, one service period, six different answers. Run the same numbers through the end-of-service rules of the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman and Bahrain — side by side.

In short: the Gulf's six gratuity systems differ in three ways — the salary base (basic only vs full wage), the yearly rate, and whether resigning cuts the amount (only Saudi Arabia and Kuwait cut it). This tool applies each country's real formula to your figures so you can see the differences. Amounts are in the currency units you enter; each country would actually pay in its own currency.

How the six rules differ

  • UAE: basic wage only; 21 days per year for 5 years, 30 days after; 1-year minimum; capped at 2 years' wage; no resignation cut.
  • Saudi Arabia: last wage including fixed allowances; half a month per year for 5 years, one month after; resignation scale 0 / ⅓ / ⅔ / full.
  • Qatar: basic wage; contract rate with a legal floor of 21 days per year (this comparison uses the floor — a 30-day contract would pay more); 1-year minimum; no resignation cut.
  • Kuwait: full wage; 15 days per year for 5 years at the ÷ 26 daily rate, one month per year after; capped at 18 months; resignation scale 0 / ½ / ⅔ / full.
  • Oman: basic wage; service before Aug 2023 at old-law rates (15 days, then one month), service after at one month per year; no resignation cut.
  • Bahrain: basic + social allowance; half a month per year for 3 years, one month after; no cap; no resignation cut.

Worked example (employer-ended): basic 10,000, total wage 15,000, 7 years → UAE 55,000 · Saudi Arabia 67,500 · Qatar 49,000 · Kuwait 73,269.23 · Oman 70,000 (if all service is under the 2023 law) · Bahrain 55,000. Switch the ending to "resigns" and only the Saudi (45,000) and Kuwait (48,846.15) figures fall.

Frequently asked questions

Which Gulf country pays the highest gratuity?

It depends on your package. Saudi Arabia and Kuwait count allowances, so they often lead when allowances are large; the UAE's 30-days-per-year rate leads on basic-heavy packages after year five. Your own split decides it — that is what the table above is for.

Why do the salary bases differ?

Each labour law defines its own base: basic only (UAE, Qatar, Oman), last wage with fixed allowances (Saudi), full wage (Kuwait), basic plus social allowance (Bahrain).

Can I compare the amounts directly?

Only as formulas. Real payouts are in six different currencies of very different value (the Kuwaiti dinar and Omani rial are worth far more per unit than the dirham or riyal). Use your country's dedicated calculator for the exact figure.

Which countries cut gratuity for resigning?

Only Saudi Arabia and Kuwait, on the scales shown above. The other four pay the same either way.

How it works — the formula

This tool runs one set of inputs through the six national formulas side by side, exactly as each country's own Loona calculator implements them. Three different salary bases are in play: the UAE, Qatar and Oman use the basic salary only; Saudi Arabia and Kuwait use the total wage (basic plus fixed allowances); Bahrain uses basic plus the social allowance. Service years come from your dates (days ÷ 365.25), and Oman is split at 1 August 2023 into old-law and new-law service.

  • UAE: basic ÷ 30 × (21 days × first 5 years + 30 days × later years); nothing under 1 year; capped at 24 months of basic salary.
  • Saudi Arabia: 0.5 month of total wage × first 5 years + 1 month × later years; Article 85 resignation scale 0 / ⅓ / ⅔ / full.
  • Qatar: basic ÷ 30 × 21 days × years — the Article 54 legal floor; nothing under 1 year.
  • Kuwait: 15 days per year for 5 years at total wage ÷ 26, then 1 month per year; Article 53 resignation scale 0 / ½ / ⅔ / full; capped at 18 months of wage.
  • Oman: pre-Aug-2023 service at 0.5 month × first 3 years + 1 month after; post-Aug-2023 service at 1 month per year — all on basic salary.
  • Bahrain: (basic + social allowance) × (0.5 × first 3 years + 1 × later years); no cap.

Worked example

Monthly basic salary 6,000, total monthly wage 9,000, no social allowance, service 1 March 2018 to 1 March 2026 — exactly 8.00 years, of which 5.42 years fall before Oman's August 2023 law change, employer ends the contract. Switch the ending to "resigns" and only two figures move: Saudi Arabia falls to 33,000.00 (the ⅔ share at 8 years) and Kuwait to 35,307.69.

Frequently asked questions

Why do the UAE, Oman and Bahrain all show 39,000 in the example above?

A coincidence of these particular inputs, not a shared formula. At 8 years the UAE day-count formula happens to total 6.5 months of basic salary, Bahrain's rate is also 6.5 months of its (here identical) wage base, and Oman's split service lands on the same total. Change the service length or the allowance mix and the three separate immediately.

I worked in two Gulf countries — can I add the two results together?

Each country's gratuity covers only the service you completed there, under that country's law and on the wage you earned there. Run the comparison once per employment, with that job's salary and dates — there is no combined GCC scheme, and service in one country does not count in another.

Why is Kuwait's daily wage the monthly wage divided by 26?

Kuwaiti practice converts a monthly wage to a daily wage over 26 working days, and Article 51's first-five-years rate is expressed in days (15 days per year). This comparison follows that convention — the same one used by our Kuwait calculator — while service after the fifth year is paid as full months.

Does the comparison include notice pay or unused leave?

No — it compares end-of-service gratuity and indemnity only. Final settlements usually contain other items (unused leave, notice pay, repatriation), which depend on the contract and are not part of any of the six formulas.

My contract promises more than the legal formula — which figure should I rely on?

The higher one. These are statutory formulas, and Qatar's is explicitly a floor (21 days per year); a contract can improve on the law but cannot lawfully pay less than it. Where your contract gives a better rate, that rate governs your actual payment.

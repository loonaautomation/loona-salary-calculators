UAE Final Settlement & How Gratuity Is Calculated

Your final settlement is everything your employer owes you when your job ends: end-of-service gratuity, pay for unused annual leave, notice pay and any unpaid salary, minus only the deductions the law allows. Gratuity is usually the largest line. This guide explains every line, shows a complete worked settlement, and covers the 2022 rule change that many older guides still get wrong.

In short: a UAE final settlement = gratuity + unused leave pay + notice pay + unpaid salary − lawful deductions, and your employer must pay it within 14 days of your contract ending. The gratuity line is 21 days of your last basic salary for each of the first five years of service and 30 days per year after that, capped at two years’ wage. You need at least one year of service, allowances do not count towards gratuity, and since February 2022 resigning does not reduce the amount.

The law in brief

End-of-service gratuity in the UAE mainland private sector is set by Article 51 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, the current Labour Law. It applies to full-time foreign (non-UAE-national) employees. UAE nationals are covered by the pension system instead, and free zones such as DIFC and ADGM run their own schemes (see the exceptions below).

The five rules that decide your amount:

  • You must complete at least one full year of continuous service. Below one year, statutory gratuity is zero.
  • Only your last basic salary counts. Housing, transport, phone and every other allowance are excluded — even if they make up most of your package.
  • Your daily basic wage is your monthly basic salary divided by 30.
  • You earn 21 days of basic wage for each of the first five years of service, and 30 days for each year after the fifth. A part-year after your first year counts pro-rata.
  • The total can never exceed two years' wage — the statutory cap of 24 monthly basic salaries.

What a UAE final settlement includes — the full checklist

Gratuity is only one line of the settlement. Under Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, a mainland private-sector settlement can include all of the following. Not every line applies to every leaver — but each line that does apply must be paid within 14 days of the contract ending (Article 53).

The single most common source of confusion is the wage basis: gratuity and leave pay are worked out on your basic wage only, while unpaid salary and notice pay use your full wage (basic plus regular allowances). A settlement that uses one wage for every line is almost certainly wrong in one direction or the other. The formula, step by step

  1. Daily wage = last monthly basic salary ÷ 30.
  2. First-five-years part = daily wage × 21 × (years of service, up to 5).
  3. Later part = daily wage × 30 × (years of service beyond 5).
  4. Add the two parts, then apply the cap: if the result is more than 24 × your monthly basic salary, you receive the cap instead.

In one line: Gratuity = (Basic ÷ 30 × 21 × first 5 years) + (Basic ÷ 30 × 30 × remaining years), capped at two years' wage. Days of unpaid absence are excluded from your service period, so keep your leave records.

Worked example 1: four years of service

Fatima's last basic salary is AED 8,000 a month and she completes exactly 4 years.

  • Daily wage: 8,000 ÷ 30 = AED 266.67
  • All 4 years fall in the first-five band: 266.67 × 21 days × 4 years = AED 22,400
  • Cap check: 24 × 8,000 = AED 192,000 — not reached.

Her gratuity is AED 22,400. Note how the answer changes if you mistakenly used her full package (say AED 14,000 with allowances): the same formula would wrongly give AED 39,200. Always check which figure your payslip calls "basic".

Worked example 2: nine years of service

Omar's last basic salary is AED 15,000 a month and he completes 9 years.

  • Daily wage: 15,000 ÷ 30 = AED 500
  • First 5 years: 500 × 21 × 5 = AED 52,500
  • Next 4 years: 500 × 30 × 4 = AED 60,000
  • Total: 52,500 + 60,000 = AED 112,500 (cap of AED 360,000 not reached)

Notice the jump in the rate after year five — each extra year beyond the fifth is worth 30 days instead of 21, roughly 43% more per year. That is why long-serving employees see their gratuity accelerate.

Worked example: a complete final settlement

Ahmed's last basic salary is AED 10,000 a month and his full wage is AED 15,000 (basic plus housing and transport allowances). He completes 6 years of service, has 12 days of unused annual leave, is owed 10 days of unpaid salary, and his employer ends the contract without requiring him to serve his 30-day notice period.

  • Gratuity (basic wage): daily basic wage = 10,000 ÷ 30 = AED 333.33. First 5 years: 333.33 × 21 × 5 = AED 35,000. Year 6: 333.33 × 30 × 1 = AED 10,000. Gratuity = AED 45,000.
  • Unused leave pay (basic wage): 333.33 × 12 days = AED 4,000.
  • Unpaid salary (full wage): daily full wage = 15,000 ÷ 30 = AED 500. 500 × 10 days = AED 5,000.
  • Notice pay (full wage): 500 × 30 days not served = AED 15,000.
  • Total final settlement: 45,000 + 4,000 + 5,000 + 15,000 = AED 69,000, payable within 14 days of the contract ending.

If Ahmed had resigned instead, his gratuity would still be AED 45,000 — resignation does not reduce it. The notice line is the one that changes with who ends the contract and whether the notice period is actually served: if you serve your full notice, you are simply paid your normal salary for those days instead.

How and when you get paid: the settlement timeline

  1. Agree the last working day. The notice period in your contract must be between 30 and 90 days (Article 43). The contract — and your service for gratuity purposes — runs until the notice period ends.
  2. Ask for an itemised settlement statement. It should show each line separately: salary arrears, gratuity, leave pay, notice pay and any deductions, each with its wage basis.
  3. Check every line before you sign. Compare the gratuity line with the formula on this page and the leave line with your actual unused days. Signing a settlement statement can be treated as accepting the figures.
  4. Payment is due within 14 days of the contract ending (Article 53), normally through the same channel as your salary.
  5. If it is late or short, act promptly. Raise the specific line in writing with your employer first; if it stays unresolved, you can file a complaint with MOHRE (the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation), which handles private-sector labour disputes on the mainland.

Deductions and common disputes

An employer may only deduct amounts the law allows (Article 25) — for example a loan or advance you agreed to repay in writing, or an amount owed under the law or a court order. An employer cannot deduct money simply because you resigned, and cannot hold back your gratuity as a penalty for leaving.

The disputes that come up most often are usually calculation differences rather than bad faith, and each has a factual answer:

  • Basic vs total salary: gratuity and leave pay use the basic wage on your contract and payslip — check which figure is labelled "basic" before disputing the total.
  • Leave days: compare the employer's leave record with your own approvals; unused days include the pro-rata leave earned in your final part-year.
  • Unpaid absence: days of unpaid absence are excluded from your service period for gratuity, so an absence record you do not recognise directly reduces the amount.
  • Notice days: count the days of notice actually served; only the unserved part is paid as notice pay.

If a line still does not match after you have checked these points, MOHRE can review the calculation — bring your contract, recent payslips and leave records.

The February 2022 change: resignation no longer cuts your gratuity

This is the point most outdated websites — and many older calculators — still get wrong. Before 2 February 2022, the UAE had two contract types, "limited" and "unlimited", and if you resigned, your gratuity could be cut to one third (early years) or two thirds (later years) of the full amount.

Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 abolished that system. Since 2 February 2022, all private-sector contracts are fixed-term, and resigning does not reduce your gratuity at all. Omar in the example above receives the same AED 112,500 whether his employer ends the contract or he resigns after nine years.

So if a calculator asks whether your contract is "limited or unlimited", it is using a law that no longer exists — close the tab. One narrow exception remains: under Article 68(3), an employer may settle a genuinely pre-2022 unlimited contract under the old law. If you signed an unlimited contract before February 2022 and never moved to a new one, ask HR or MOHRE which basis applies to you.

Who is not covered by this formula

  • DIFC and ADGM employees: these financial free zones have their own employment laws and workplace savings schemes (such as DEWS in DIFC) instead of Article 51 gratuity.
  • Domestic workers: covered by a separate law (Federal Decree-Law No. 9 of 2022) with its own end-of-service rules.
  • UAE nationals: covered by the federal pension system, not gratuity.
  • Employees in the alternative savings scheme: some employers have switched staff to the voluntary investment-based savings scheme that replaces gratuity with monthly fund contributions. If your employer enrolled you, your entitlement follows the scheme, not this formula.

When you leave, Article 53 requires your employer to pay all end-of-service entitlements within 14 days of the contract ending. If the amount looks wrong, compare it line by line with the formula above before signing any settlement statement.

Frequently asked questions

Does resigning reduce my UAE gratuity?

No. Since 2 February 2022, resignation has no effect on gratuity. The old one-third and two-thirds reductions belonged to the abolished limited/unlimited contract system.

Is gratuity calculated on my basic salary or my total salary?

On your last basic salary only. Allowances such as housing and transport are excluded, and the daily rate is the monthly basic divided by 30.

I worked 11 months. Do I get anything?

Under Article 51, no — gratuity starts after one full year of continuous service. Some employers pay pro-rata amounts voluntarily, but it is not a statutory right in the first year.

When must my employer pay my gratuity?

Within 14 days of the contract ending (Article 53), together with any other end-of-service entitlements.

What does a UAE final settlement include?

Unpaid salary up to your last working day, end-of-service gratuity, pay for unused annual leave, notice pay if the notice period was not served, and any other agreed dues — minus only the deductions the law allows (Article 25).

How is unused annual leave paid in a final settlement?

You are paid for every accrued leave day you did not take, including the pro-rata days of your final part-year, calculated on your basic wage (Article 29).

How is notice pay calculated?

The notice period in your contract must be between 30 and 90 days. If the notice period — or part of it — is not served, the party who did not observe it pays the other the full wage for the days not served (Article 43).

What can my employer deduct from my final settlement?

Only deductions the law allows under Article 25 — for example a loan or advance you agreed in writing to repay, or an amount owed under the law or a court order. Resigning is never a lawful reason to deduct or withhold gratuity.

Does a part-year of service count towards gratuity?

Yes, once you have completed your first full year. A part-year after that counts pro-rata at the rate for that band — 21 days per year in the first five years, 30 days per year after the fifth.

What if my employer does not pay within 14 days?

Raise the unpaid line in writing with your employer first. If it stays unresolved, you can file a complaint with MOHRE, the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation, which handles mainland private-sector labour disputes.

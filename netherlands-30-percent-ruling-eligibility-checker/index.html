30% Ruling Eligibility Checker

Five tests decide whether you can get the Netherlands 30% ruling. Answer honestly and this checker walks through each one — the salary norm, the 150 km rule, recruitment from abroad, and the employer conditions.

In short: you qualify for the 30% ruling if you were recruited from abroad, lived more than 150 km from the Dutch border for at least 16 of the last 24 months, work for a Dutch wage-tax-withholding employer, and your taxable salary after the allowance stays above €48,013 in 2026 (€36,497 if under 30 with a master's; researchers are exempt from the salary test). A salary between the norm and about €68,600 still qualifies, with a reduced allowance.

The five tests, explained

  • Salary norm (the expertise test): your taxable salary after the tax-free allowance must exceed €48,013 in 2026 — or €36,497 if you are under 30 with a master's degree from a recognised university. Researchers and doctors in specialist training skip this test. If the full 30% would pull you under the norm, the allowance is reduced instead — you still qualify.
  • 150 km rule: for at least 16 of the 24 months before your first working day, you lived more than 150 km in a straight line from the Dutch border.
  • Recruited from abroad: you were hired (or seconded) while living outside the Netherlands. Moving first and job-hunting afterwards normally fails this test.
  • Employer: your employer must withhold Dutch wage tax, and you must both agree the ruling in writing.
  • Application: your employer applies to the Belastingdienst — within 4 months of your start date for the ruling to count from day one. It then runs for up to 5 years (less any earlier Dutch residence or work periods).

Worked examples: a €100,000 offer passes the salary test with the full €30,000 allowance. A €60,000 offer passes with a reduced allowance of €11,987. A €40,000 standard-norm offer fails the salary test, while the same €40,000 under the under-30 master's norm passes with a €3,503 allowance.

Frequently asked questions

What are the requirements for the 30% ruling?

Recruitment from abroad, the 150 km rule, a Dutch withholding employer, a written agreement, and a taxable salary above the norm after the allowance — €48,013 in 2026 (€36,497 under-30 master's; researchers exempt).

My salary drops below the norm after 30% — do I still qualify?

Usually yes, with a reduced allowance: the allowance is cut so your taxable salary stays above the norm. On €60,000, that means an €11,987 allowance instead of €18,000.

What is the 150 km rule?

You must have lived more than 150 km from the Dutch border for at least 16 of the 24 months before starting. Living just over the Belgian or German border usually fails it.

When must it be applied for?

Your employer applies to the Belastingdienst; within 4 months of your start date it can take effect from day one. Duration: up to 5 years.

Researchers — does the salary norm apply?

No. Scientific researchers and doctors in specialist training are exempt from the salary test; the other conditions still apply.

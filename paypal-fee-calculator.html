How it is calculated

PayPal's fee on a commercial payment is a percentage of the amount plus a small fixed fee: fee = amount × % + fixed fee. At the standard US commercial rate of 2.99% + $0.49, a $100 payment costs $3.48, so you receive $96.52. To land on an exact amount after the fee you have to work backwards — the “gross-up”: amount to request = (amount you want + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − % ÷ 100).

Frequently asked questions

What is PayPal's standard fee for receiving money?

For US commercial transactions — payments for goods and services — the standard PayPal rate is 2.99% of the amount plus a fixed fee of $0.49. So a $100 payment leaves you $96.52. Other PayPal products (checkout, invoicing paid by card, micropayments) and other countries use different rates; every rate in this calculator is editable.

How do I make sure I receive an exact amount after PayPal fees?

Use the second mode of this calculator. It grosses the amount up with the formula (target + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − fee %), so the fee comes out of the requested amount and your exact target remains. For $100 at the standard US rate you request $103.59 — a simple “add 3%” guess would leave you about ten cents short.

Why is the fee more than 3% on small payments?

Because of the fixed $0.49 part. On a $5 payment the fee is $0.49 + about $0.15 — nearly 13% of the payment. The smaller the payment, the bigger the fixed fee looms, which is why this calculator always shows the fee and the net side by side.

What does PayPal charge for international payments?

International commercial payments add a percentage on top of the domestic rate (1.5% for US accounts) and, if a currency conversion is involved, a conversion margin of several percent above the market rate. Add the extra percentage to the fee field above to model it — for example 2.99% + 1.5% = 4.49%, plus the $0.49 fixed fee.

“Friends and family” has no fee — can I use it for sales?

No. Personal “friends and family” payments are for money between people who know each other, not for goods and services; there is no purchase protection, and using them to dodge fees on business sales breaches PayPal's rules and can get an account limited. This calculator uses the commercial rates that apply to real sales.

How it works — the formula

In “receiving” mode the calculator applies fee = amount × fee % ÷ 100 + fixed fee, then net = amount − fee. In “receive an exact amount” mode it reverses the same formula: amount to request = (target + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − fee % ÷ 100), so the fee on that larger request leaves exactly the target. Results are rounded to two decimals for display, and the request amount is rounded to the nearest cent — on a cent boundary the fee can differ by a cent, which the result table shows honestly.

Frequently asked questions

Does PayPal refund its fee when I refund a buyer?

Generally no — since PayPal's policy change, the original transaction fee is kept when a commercial payment is refunded (with limited exceptions). That makes the fee part of your cost even on returned sales, and it is one reason sellers check the numbers with a calculator like this before pricing.

Why does the amount I request in gross-up mode look odd, like $103.59?

Because the fee is charged on the requested amount itself, including the top-up. Asking for $103.00 would leave the fee to be taken out of that too: $103.00 − ($3.08 + $0.49) = $99.43. The gross-up formula solves for the exact amount whose fee leaves precisely your target.

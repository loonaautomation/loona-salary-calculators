How it is calculated

  • Social contributions first (employee shares, each capped): pension 9.3% up to €101,400/year · unemployment 1.3% up to €101,400 · health 7.3% + half your insurer's supplementary contribution (2.9% average in 2026 → 8.75% total) up to €69,750 · long-term care 1.8% up to €69,750 — 2.4% if childless and 23+, less 0.25 points per child from the second child under 25. (In Saxony the care split differs slightly.)
  • Taxable income ≈ gross − employee social contributions − €1,230 employee lump sum − €36 special-expenses lump sum. This is the standard simplification used by most calculators; official payroll uses the tax office's detailed programmed flow, so treat results as an estimate.
  • Income tax follows the §32a tariff for 2026: no tax up to the basic allowance of €12,348, then progressive zones rising to a 42% marginal rate from €69,879, and 45% above €277,826. Tax classes adjust the allowance: III gets a double allowance, II an extra €4,260 relief, V and VI no allowance. Classes affect withholding only, never your final annual bill.
  • Solidarity surcharge: 5.5% of the income tax, but only once the tax passes roughly €20,350 (single) / €40,700 (class III), phased in through a mitigation zone — most employees pay €0.
  • Church tax: 8% (Bavaria, Baden-Württemberg) or 9% (elsewhere) of the income tax, only for registered church members.

Worked example: €60,000 gross, class I, one child, no church tax → social contributions €12,690, income tax ≈ €9,186, soli €0 → net ≈ €38,124 per year (€3,177 per month).

Frequently asked questions

What are the tax classes?

I single · II single parent (+€4,260 relief) · III married higher earner (double allowance) · IV married, similar incomes · V married lower earner (no allowance) · VI second job. They change monthly withholding, not the final annual tax.

Do I pay the solidarity surcharge?

Probably not: it only starts above roughly €20,350 of annual income tax (2026, single), with a phase-in zone. Most employees pay €0.

Which contributions come out of my salary?

Pension 9.3%, unemployment 1.3%, health about 8.75% (at the average supplementary rate) and care 1.8–2.4% — each capped, roughly 20–21% of gross in total.

Why 8% vs 9% church tax?

Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg charge 8% of income tax; every other state charges 9%. Only registered members of a tax-collecting church pay it.

Is the result exact?

It is a documented estimate: official payroll follows the BMF's programmed flow in more detail. Small differences from a real payslip are normal — the structure and the big numbers are right.

UAE Gratuity Calculator

Work out your end-of-service gratuity under Article 51 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 — the current law. Note: resigning does not reduce your gratuity (that rule ended in February 2022), so there is deliberately no contract-type question here.

In short: UAE gratuity is 21 days of your last basic salary per year for the first five years of service and 30 days per year after that, capped at two years’ wage. You need at least one year of service, allowances do not count, and since February 2022 resigning does not reduce it.

How it is calculated

Article 51 of the UAE Labour Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021) sets gratuity for full-time foreign private-sector workers as follows:

  • You must complete at least 1 year of continuous service; below that, gratuity is AED 0.
  • Only your basic salary counts — housing, transport and other allowances are excluded. Daily basic wage = monthly basic ÷ 30.
  • 21 days of basic wage for each of the first 5 years, then 30 days per year after the fifth. Part-years are pro-rated.
  • The total is capped at 2 years' wage.
  • Formula: Gratuity = (Basic ÷ 30 × 21 × first-5-years) + (Basic ÷ 30 × 30 × years after 5), capped at 24 × monthly basic.

Worked example: basic salary AED 10,000, 7 years of service → (10,000 ÷ 30 × 21 × 5) + (10,000 ÷ 30 × 30 × 2) = AED 35,000 + AED 20,000 = AED 55,000.

Not covered: DIFC and ADGM free zones have their own employment schemes; domestic workers fall under a separate law; UAE nationals are covered by the pension system instead. Days of unpaid absence are excluded from service.

Frequently asked questions

Does resigning reduce my UAE gratuity?

No. Since 2 February 2022, resignation does not reduce gratuity. The old limited/unlimited contract reductions were abolished — any calculator asking for your contract type is using the old law.

Is gratuity on basic salary or full salary?

Basic salary only. Daily basic wage = last monthly basic ÷ 30. Allowances never count.

What if I worked less than one year?

Statutory gratuity is zero below one year of continuous service. After that, part-years count pro-rata.

What is the maximum?

Two years' wage in total. This calculator applies the cap automatically and shows it in the breakdown.

When must my employer pay?

Within 14 days of the contract ending (Article 53).

I had a pre-2022 unlimited contract — anything different?

Possibly: Article 68(3) lets an employer settle a legacy unlimited contract under the old law. Check with HR or MOHRE if that describes you.

How it works — the formula

Under Article 51 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, the calculator takes your last monthly basic salary and divides it by 30 to get the daily basic wage. Gratuity = daily wage × 21 × years in the first five years, plus daily wage × 30 × years after the fifth — then the result is capped at two years' wage (24 × monthly basic), and service under one year pays zero. Your service is measured from the two dates you enter, in days ÷ 365.25, so part-years are pro-rated automatically rather than rounded down to completed years.

Worked example

Last basic salary AED 12,000, 8 years of service. Daily basic wage = 12,000 ÷ 30 = AED 400. First five years: 400 × 21 × 5 = AED 42,000. The three years after the fifth: 400 × 30 × 3 = AED 36,000. Total gratuity = AED 78,000 — comfortably below the AED 288,000 cap for this salary.

Frequently asked questions

Do part-years count, or only completed years?

Part-years count. Because service is measured in days ÷ 365.25, 8 years and 6 months is paid as 8.5 years — the extra half year at the 30-days-per-year rate that applies after year five.

When does the two-year cap actually bite?

Only on very long service. On a basic salary of AED 10,000 the uncapped formula reaches AED 285,000 after 30 years, but the amount paid is the cap: AED 240,000. Below roughly 25 years of service the cap does not bind at that salary.

My basic salary rose over the years — which one is used?

Only your last monthly basic salary. The entire service is valued at that final rate, so a late promotion lifts the gratuity for every year you worked, not just the last one.

Does this calculator deduct unpaid absence?

No — it measures the calendar time between the dates you enter. The law excludes days of unpaid absence from service, so if you had a long unpaid break, shorten the service dates you enter to match.

Does this cover DIFC or ADGM employment?

No. DIFC and ADGM run their own end-of-service schemes instead of Article 51. This calculator applies the mainland federal law only.

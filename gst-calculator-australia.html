How it is calculated

GST in Australia is charged at 10% on most taxable supplies. The two formulas are:

  • Add GST: GST = Net × 10 ÷ 100, and Gross = Net + GST.
  • Remove GST: Net = Gross ÷ (1 + 10 ÷ 100), and GST = Gross − Net.
  • Standard rate: 10%. Other rates: a single flat rate of 10% — GST-free and input-taxed categories apply instead (see the FAQ).

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard GST rate in Australia?

The standard GST rate in Australia is 10%. It applies to most goods, services and other items sold or consumed in Australia and has been 10% since GST was introduced on 1 July 2000. GST is called Goods and Services Tax (GST) locally, amounts on this page are in Australian dollars (AUD), and the rates here were verified in November 2026 against Australian Taxation Office (ATO) — GST rates and definitions.

What is GST-free or input-taxed in Australia?

GST-free supplies include most basic foods, many health and medical services, education, childcare, exports and charitable activities — no GST is charged, but the seller can still claim GST credits on inputs. Input-taxed supplies (financial supplies and residential rent) charge no GST and the seller generally cannot claim credits on related purchases.

How do I add GST to a price in Australia?

Multiply the net price by 0.1 to get the GST amount at the 10% standard rate, then add it to the net price — or use the Add GST mode above. For example, on a net price of A$ 100.00 the GST is A$ 10.00 and the gross price is A$ 110.00.

How do I remove GST from a gross price in Australia?

Divide the gross price by 1.1 to get the net amount at the 10% standard rate, then subtract the net from the gross to get the GST — or use the Remove GST mode above. For example, a gross price of A$ 100.00 contains A$ 9.09 of GST, leaving a net amount of A$ 90.91.

How do I find the GST in an Australian price quickly?

Divide the GST-inclusive price by 11. Because GST is 10% of the net price, the GST inside a gross price is exactly one-eleventh of it: a $110.00 price contains $10.00 of GST and a $100.00 net. The Remove GST mode above does the same calculation for any amount.

How it works — the formula

The calculator stores no data and does one piece of arithmetic per mode. In Add GST mode it treats your amount as the net price: it multiplies the net by the chosen rate divided by 100 to get the GST, then adds the two together for the gross price you charge. In Remove GST mode it treats your amount as a gross, GST-inclusive price — the kind printed on a Australia receipt or shelf label — and divides it by (1 + rate ÷ 100) to recover the net amount; the difference between the gross and the net is the GST contained in the price. Because consumer prices in Australia must be displayed GST-inclusive and business prices are often quoted either way, the two modes are exact inverses: removing GST from a gross figure and then adding it back returns the original amount, apart from rounding to two decimal places in AUD.

Frequently asked questions

What must a tax invoice in Australia show?

For sales over $82.50 (including GST), a tax invoice must show that it is a tax invoice, the seller's identity and ABN, the issue date, a brief description of what was sold, the GST amount (or a statement that the total includes GST) and, for invoices of $1,000 or more, the buyer's identity or ABN.

I sell from Australia to customers overseas — is GST charged?

Generally no: exports of goods and many services supplied to overseas customers are GST-free, provided the export conditions and time limits are met. Low-value imported goods bought by Australian consumers are instead taxed at the border or by the overseas seller under the imported-services and low-value-goods rules.

Are consumer prices in Australia shown including GST?

Yes. The price displayed to consumers must be the total price including GST, so use the Remove GST mode to find the net amount inside a shelf price. In business quotes, watch for 'plus GST' wording — that means the quoted figure is net and GST must be added.

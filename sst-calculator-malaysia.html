Malaysia SST Calculator

Add or remove Malaysian SST: service tax at the standard 8% rate (6% for the categories that kept the old rate), or sales tax at 10% / 5%. The default is the 8% service tax. Amounts are in Malaysian ringgit (MYR) and results update as you type.

In short: Malaysia does not have GST — it replaced GST with SST on 1 September 2018. SST is two single-stage taxes: service tax (8% since 1 March 2024; 6% for categories such as food & beverage and telecommunications that kept the lower rate) charged by registered service providers, and sales tax (10% standard, 5% for specified goods) charged once, at manufacturer or importer level. Unlike GST/VAT there is no input-tax credit chain — the tax simply forms part of the price, which is why the same add/remove maths below is what businesses and consumers actually use.

How it is calculated

SST in Malaysia is charged at the rate for the tax type — service tax 8% standard since 1 March 2024. The two formulas are:

  • Add SST: SST = Net × 8 ÷ 100, and Gross = Net + SST.
  • Remove SST: Net = Gross ÷ (1 + 8 ÷ 100), and SST = Gross − Net.
  • Standard rate: 8%. Other rates: service tax 6% (kept categories), sales tax 10% and 5% — all in the rate selector above.

Which rate do I use? If you are a customer or provider of a taxable service, use 8% (or 6% for food & beverage, telecommunications, parking and logistics services). Sales tax (10% / 5%) is charged by manufacturers and importers on goods and is normally already inside the price of goods you buy.

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard SST rate in Malaysia?

The standard SST rate in Malaysia is 8%. If you mean service tax, the standard rate is 8% (raised from 6% on 1 March 2024). If you mean sales tax, the standard rate is 10%. The two taxes never apply to the same transaction — pick the one that matches your supply in the rate selector. SST is called Sales Tax and Service Tax (SST) locally, amounts on this page are in Malaysian ringgit (MYR), and the rates here were verified in November 2026 against Royal Malaysian Customs Department (JKDM) — Sales Tax Act 2018 and Service Tax Act 2018 rates.

What is the difference between sales tax and service tax?

Sales tax is a single-stage tax on goods: charged by registered manufacturers on their sales and by Customs on imports, at 10% generally or 5% for specified goods (such as certain foods, building materials and machinery), with many basic foods exempt. Service tax is charged by registered providers of listed taxable services — hotels, restaurants, professional services, digital services and more — at 8%, with some categories (food and beverage, telecommunications, parking, logistics) kept at 6% when the rate rose in March 2024. Neither tax has an input-credit mechanism, unlike the GST Malaysia had from 2015 to 2018.

How do I add SST to a price in Malaysia?

Multiply the net price by 0.08 to get the SST amount at the 8% standard rate, then add it to the net price — or use the Add SST mode above. For example, on a net price of RM 100.00 the SST is RM 8.00 and the gross price is RM 108.00.

How do I remove SST from a gross price in Malaysia?

Divide the gross price by 1.08 to get the net amount at the 8% standard rate, then subtract the net from the gross to get the SST — or use the Remove SST mode above. For example, a gross price of RM 100.00 contains RM 7.41 of SST, leaving a net amount of RM 92.59.

Does SST work like GST or VAT?

No. GST/VAT is charged at every stage with credits for tax paid on inputs, so the net cost to registered businesses is zero. SST is single-stage: sales tax is charged once (manufacturer/importer) and service tax once (the provider bills the customer), and businesses cannot claim credits — the tax embeds in prices. Registration thresholds also differ by service category, administered by the Royal Malaysian Customs Department (JKDM).

How it works — the formula

The calculator stores no data and does one piece of arithmetic per mode. In Add SST mode it treats your amount as the net price: it multiplies the net by the chosen rate divided by 100 to get the SST, then adds the two together for the gross price you charge. In Remove SST mode it treats your amount as a gross, SST-inclusive price — the kind printed on a Malaysia receipt or shelf label — and divides it by (1 + rate ÷ 100) to recover the net amount; the difference between the gross and the net is the SST contained in the price. The two modes are exact inverses for price maths — removing SST from a tax-inclusive figure and adding it back returns the original amount, apart from rounding to two decimal places in MYR. Remember, though, that SST itself is single-stage: unlike VAT there is no credit for SST paid on your own inputs.

Frequently asked questions

What must an SST invoice in Malaysia show?

A service tax invoice from a registered provider shows the provider's name, address and SST registration number, the invoice number and date, a description of the service, the amount excluding tax, the service tax rate and amount, and the total in MYR. Sales tax is shown on manufacturers' and importers' invoices under the Sales Tax Act rules.

I sell services from abroad to Malaysians — does SST apply?

Foreign providers of digital services to Malaysian consumers must register for service tax and charge 8% (the imported taxable services regime). Malaysian businesses importing services self-account for service tax under the reverse-charge-style rules for imported taxable services.

Are consumer prices in Malaysia shown including SST?

Where service tax applies, the displayed price for consumers is normally the tax-inclusive total, so use the Remove mode to split out the net and the tax. For goods, sales tax is charged earlier in the chain and is simply part of the shelf price — you cannot strip it out of a retail receipt the way you can with VAT.

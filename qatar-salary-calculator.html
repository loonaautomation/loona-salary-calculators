Qatar Net Salary Calculator

Gross to net in Qatar is the simplest in the world: Qatar charges no personal income tax at all. Expatriate employees keep their full gross salary; Qatari citizens pay a 7% social insurance contribution (GRSIA).

In short: Qatar has no personal income tax — a QAR 300,000.00 gross salary is QAR 300,000.00 net for an expatriate employee, with nothing deducted. Qatari citizens pay 7% into the GRSIA pension fund. Your other big end-of-job benefit is the end-of-service gratuity, calculated separately.

How it is calculated

  • Income tax: Qatar levies no tax on employment income — for expatriates and citizens alike, the income tax line is always zero. (Qatar does tax companies, including a 10% corporate rate, but that never touches your payslip.)
  • Social insurance (GRSIA): only Qatari nationals (and GCC nationals under reciprocal rules) contribute: 7% of pensionable salary from the employee, 14% from the employer, paid to the General Retirement and Social Insurance Authority. Expatriate employees are outside the scheme and pay nothing.
  • End-of-service gratuity: instead of a pension, expatriates earn a gratuity — at least three weeks of basic salary for every year of service under the Qatar Labour Law — paid when the job ends. It is a lump sum on top of salary, not a deduction, so it is not in this calculator: use the Qatar Gratuity Calculator linked below.
  • Wage Protection System: salaries must be paid monthly through the WPS bank-transfer system, in Qatari riyals.

Worked example: an expatriate on QAR 300,000.00 gross keeps the full QAR 300,000.00 — QAR 25,000.00 a month, zero deductions. A Qatari citizen on the same salary pays 7% GRSIA and nets QAR 279,000 a year (QAR 23,250 a month).

Frequently asked questions

Is there income tax in Qatar?

No. Qatar charges no personal income tax on salaries or any other employment income, for expatriates and Qatari citizens alike. Your gross salary is your starting net salary; the only possible payslip deduction is the 7% GRSIA social insurance contribution, and that applies to Qatari (and GCC) nationals only.

Do expatriates pay social insurance in Qatar?

No. The GRSIA pension scheme covers Qatari nationals (and GCC citizens under reciprocal arrangements). Expatriate employees make no social insurance contributions in Qatar, so their net salary equals their gross salary.

What is the end-of-service gratuity in Qatar?

Expatriates receive an end-of-service gratuity instead of a pension: at least three weeks of basic salary for each year of service, under Article 54 of the Qatar Labour Law, paid when employment ends. It is calculated on your basic salary, not your total package — work yours out with our Qatar Gratuity Calculator.

Are housing and transport allowances taxed in Qatar?

No — because there is no income tax at all, every part of your package (basic salary, housing allowance, transport allowance, bonuses) is paid in full. Allowances do matter for the gratuity, which is based on basic salary only.

How is salary paid in Qatar?

Through the Wage Protection System (WPS): your employer must transfer your salary to a Qatari bank account, in Qatari riyals, at least monthly. Payment in cash outside the WPS is not compliant.

How it works — the formula

Net = gross − income tax − social insurance. In Qatar the income tax term is always zero, and the social insurance term is zero for expatriates, so the formula collapses to net = gross for most of Qatar's workforce. For a Qatari citizen the only term is GRSIA: 7% of pensionable salary, so net = gross × 0.93. Employer contributions (14% for citizens) are paid on top of your salary and never reduce your net pay.

Worked example

Expatriate, gross QAR 300,000.00 a year. Income tax: QAR 0. Social insurance: QAR 0 (expatriates are outside GRSIA). Net = QAR 300,000.00 a year — QAR 25,000.00 a month, exactly the gross figure. A Qatari citizen on the same salary pays 7% × QAR 300,000.00 = QAR 21,000 into GRSIA and nets QAR 279,000 a year (QAR 23,250 a month).

Frequently asked questions

Are employer GRSIA contributions deducted from my pay?

No. For Qatari citizens the employer pays its own 14% GRSIA contribution on top of your salary. Only the 7% employee share ever appears on a payslip.

I am a GCC national working in Qatar — what applies to me?

Under the GCC insurance protection extension system you stay in your home country's pension scheme, and your Qatari employer registers you there. Your deduction follows your home scheme's employee rate, not Qatar's 7%.

Does Qatar tax bonuses or end-of-service payments?

No. With no personal income tax, bonuses, allowances and the end-of-service gratuity are all paid without tax. The gratuity is worked out on basic salary — see the Qatar Gratuity Calculator.

Why do job offers in Qatar split salary into basic and allowances?

Because the end-of-service gratuity (and some leave payments) are calculated on the basic salary only. A package of QAR 30,000 with a QAR 12,000 basic builds gratuity much more slowly than one with a QAR 20,000 basic, even though the monthly net is identical.

Qatar Gratuity Calculator

End-of-service gratuity under Article 54 of the Qatari Labour Law — which sets a legal floor of 21 days per year, not a fixed rate. If your contract promises more, enter your contract's days below.

How it is calculated

Article 54 of Labour Law No. 14 of 2004 works differently from its Gulf neighbours: it guarantees a minimum, and your contract sets the actual rate.

  • You must complete at least 1 year of service; below that, gratuity is QAR 0.
  • The floor is 3 weeks' wage — 21 days — per year of service, on your last basic wage. Daily wage = monthly basic ÷ 30.
  • Formula (legal minimum): Gratuity = (Basic ÷ 30) × 21 × years of service — the same as 0.7 × monthly basic per year.
  • The rate does not rise after 5 years in the law itself — but many contracts voluntarily give 30 days per year, and the contract wins.
  • No resignation reduction: the amount is the same whether you resign or the employer ends the contract.
  • Part-years are pro-rated. Domestic workers are under a separate law (No. 15 of 2017, same 3-week minimum); government civil staff are under a different law again.

Worked example: basic salary QAR 10,000, 5 years of service at the legal minimum → (10,000 ÷ 30) × 21 × 5 = QAR 35,000. The same service on a 30-day contract rate would pay QAR 50,000.

Frequently asked questions

How is gratuity calculated in Qatar?

Article 54 sets a floor: at least 21 days of basic wage per year of service — (basic ÷ 30) × 21 × years. Your contract can give more, never less.

Is it on basic salary or full salary?

Last basic wage only, unless your contract specifically counts allowances.

Does resigning reduce Qatar gratuity?

No. There is no resignation reduction in Article 54.

My contract gives 30 days per year — which applies?

Your contract's 30 days, because it beats the legal floor. Enter 30 in the days-per-year field.

What if I worked less than one year?

No gratuity is payable; after one year, part-years count pro-rata.

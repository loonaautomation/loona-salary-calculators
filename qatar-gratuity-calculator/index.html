How it is calculated

Article 54 of Labour Law No. 14 of 2004 works differently from its Gulf neighbours: it guarantees a minimum, and your contract sets the actual rate.

  • You must complete at least 1 year of service; below that, gratuity is QAR 0.
  • The floor is 3 weeks' wage — 21 days — per year of service, on your last basic wage. Daily wage = monthly basic ÷ 30.
  • Formula (legal minimum): Gratuity = (Basic ÷ 30) × 21 × years of service — the same as 0.7 × monthly basic per year.
  • The rate does not rise after 5 years in the law itself — but many contracts voluntarily give 30 days per year, and the contract wins.
  • No resignation reduction: the amount is the same whether you resign or the employer ends the contract.
  • Part-years are pro-rated. Domestic workers are under a separate law (No. 15 of 2017, same 3-week minimum); government civil staff are under a different law again.

Worked example: basic salary QAR 10,000, 5 years of service at the legal minimum → (10,000 ÷ 30) × 21 × 5 = QAR 35,000. The same service on a 30-day contract rate would pay QAR 50,000.

Frequently asked questions

How is gratuity calculated in Qatar?

Article 54 sets a floor: at least 21 days of basic wage per year of service — (basic ÷ 30) × 21 × years. Your contract can give more, never less.

Is it on basic salary or full salary?

Last basic wage only, unless your contract specifically counts allowances.

Does resigning reduce Qatar gratuity?

No. There is no resignation reduction in Article 54.

My contract gives 30 days per year — which applies?

Your contract's 30 days, because it beats the legal floor. Enter 30 in the days-per-year field.

What if I worked less than one year?

No gratuity is payable; after one year, part-years count pro-rata.

How it works — the formula

Article 54 of Labour Law No. 14 of 2004 sets a floor, not a fixed rate: gratuity = (last monthly basic ÷ 30) × days per year × years of service, where days per year is at least 21 (three weeks) and rises to whatever your contract promises — enter your contract's rate in the days field if it is higher. Service is measured from your joining and last working dates in days ÷ 365.25, so part-years are paid pro-rata. Unlike its Gulf neighbours there is no increase after five years and no cap in the formula, and at least one year of service is required before anything is payable.

Worked example

Last basic salary QAR 15,000, 6 years of service. Daily basic wage = 15,000 ÷ 30 = QAR 500. At the legal minimum of 21 days per year: 500 × 21 × 6 = QAR 63,000. The same service on a contract that promises 30 days per year: 500 × 30 × 6 = QAR 90,000 — which is why checking your contract's rate is worth QAR 27,000 here.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a maximum on Qatar gratuity?

No statutory cap applies in the Article 54 calculation used here: the amount simply grows with your basic salary, your contract's days-per-year rate and your length of service.

How are part-years counted?

Pro-rata. Service is measured in days and divided by 365.25, so 6 years and 6 months of service is paid as roughly 6.5 years at your days-per-year rate.

What should I enter in the days-per-year field?

Your contract's promised rate if it states one (30 is common). If your contract is silent, leave it at 21 — the legal minimum under Article 54, below which no contract may go.

Does unpaid absence change the result?

This calculator measures calendar service between the two dates you enter and does not deduct absences. If your employer excludes a long unpaid absence from your service, adjust the dates you enter to match.

How does Qatar compare with the UAE formula?

The UAE pays 21 days per year for the first five years, then 30 days per year, capped at two years' wage. Qatar stays on one rate — your contract's, minimum 21 days — for every year of service, with no cap in the calculation.

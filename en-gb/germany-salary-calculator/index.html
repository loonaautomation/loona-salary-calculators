Germany Gross-to-Net Salary Calculator (in English)

Brutto to Netto for 2026, explained in English: income tax by tax class, the solidarity surcharge, optional church tax, and all four employee social insurances — each one itemised so you can see exactly where your salary goes.

In short: German employees pay four social contributions totalling roughly 20–21% of gross pay (each capped), plus income tax under the statutory §32a formula — nothing up to €12,348, rising to a 42% marginal rate from €69,879 and 45% above €277,826 in 2026. Most employees pay no solidarity surcharge, and church tax (8–9% of the income tax) applies only to registered church members.

How it is calculated

  • Social contributions first (employee shares, each capped): pension 9.3% up to €101,400/year · unemployment 1.3% up to €101,400 · health 7.3% + half your insurer's supplementary contribution (2.9% average in 2026 → 8.75% total) up to €69,750 · long-term care 1.8% up to €69,750 — 2.4% if childless and 23+, less 0.25 points per child from the second child under 25. (In Saxony the care split differs slightly.)
  • Taxable income ≈ gross − employee social contributions − €1,230 employee lump sum − €36 special-expenses lump sum. This is the standard simplification used by most calculators; official payroll computes a detailed pension-relief amount (Vorsorgepauschale) instead, so the final net remains an estimate even though the tax tariff applied to it is exact.
  • Income tax is computed with the exact statutory tariff formula of §32a EStG for 2026, taken from the official law text: no tax up to the basic allowance of €12,348, then two progressive formula zones rising to a 42% marginal rate from €69,879, and 45% above €277,826. Tax classes: III uses the statutory income-splitting procedure (the joint income is taxed as two halves), II gets an extra €4,260 single-parent relief, and V and VI are modelled without the personal allowance. Classes affect withholding only, never your final annual bill.
  • Solidarity surcharge: 5.5% of the income tax, but only once the tax passes €20,350 (single) / €40,700 (joint assessment, e.g. class III), phased in through a mitigation zone — most employees pay €0.
  • Church tax: 8% (Bavaria, Baden-Württemberg) or 9% (elsewhere) of the income tax, only for registered church members.

Worked example: €60,000 gross, class I, one child, no church tax → social contributions €12,690, income tax €9,186 (the exact §32a tariff on €46,044 of taxable income), soli €0 → net €38,124 per year (€3,177 per month).

Frequently asked questions

What are the tax classes?

I single · II single parent (+€4,260 relief) · III married higher earner (income splitting) · IV married, similar incomes · V married lower earner (no allowance) · VI second job. They change monthly withholding, not the final annual tax.

Do I pay the solidarity surcharge?

Probably not: it only starts above €20,350 of annual income tax (2026, single; €40,700 for joint assessment), with a phase-in zone. Most employees pay €0.

Which contributions come out of my salary?

Pension 9.3%, unemployment 1.3%, health about 8.75% (at the average supplementary rate) and care 1.8–2.4% — each capped, roughly 20–21% of gross in total.

Why 8% vs 9% church tax?

Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg charge 8% of income tax; every other state charges 9%. Only registered members of a tax-collecting church pay it.

Is the result exact?

The wage tax is computed with the exact statutory §32a formula for 2026, including income splitting for class III. The net pay remains a careful estimate, because payroll derives taxable income in more detail (the Vorsorgepauschale) than this tool's lump-sum simplification — small differences from a real payslip are normal, but the structure and the big numbers are right.

How it works — the formula

The calculator first deducts your employee social contributions: pension 9.3% and unemployment 1.3% on gross up to €101,400 a year; health at 7.3% plus half your insurer's supplementary contribution, and long-term care at your personal rate, both on gross up to €69,750. It then approximates taxable income as gross − social contributions − €1,230 (employee lump sum) − €36 (special-expenses lump sum), and applies the exact statutory tariff of §32a EStG for 2026 — with your tax class adjusting it (splitting in class III, the €4,260 single-parent relief in class II, no basic allowance in classes V and VI). Solidarity surcharge and church tax are added only where they apply. Because the taxable-income step uses lump sums instead of payroll's detailed pension-relief calculation, the net is an estimate — the tariff itself is exact.

Worked example

Gross €45,000 a year, tax class IV, two children under 25, no church tax, health Zusatzbeitrag 2.9%. Social contributions: pension €4,185.00 + unemployment €585.00 + health €3,937.50 + long-term care €697.50 (1.55% — the rate falls by 0.25 points for the second child) = €9,405.00. Taxable income ≈ 45,000 − 9,405 − 1,230 − 36 = €34,329, on which the §32a tariff gives income tax of €5,470; soli €0. Net pay: €30,125.00 a year — €2,510.42 a month.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the number of children change my care-insurance rate?

Long-term care (Pflegeversicherung) is the only contribution with family adjustments. The standard employee rate used here is 1.8%; parents pay 0.25 points less for each child from the second to the fifth under 25 (down to 0.8%), while employees who are childless and aged 23 or over pay a 0.6-point surcharge (2.4%). On a €45,000 salary the childless surcharge alone is €270 a year, before the small tax knock-on.

At what salary does the solidarity surcharge actually start?

Soli is 5.5% of your income tax, but only once that tax passes €20,350 a year (single) or €40,700 (joint assessment, e.g. class III), and it phases in gradually, capped at 11.9% of the excess. At €45,000 in class IV the tax is €5,470, so soli is €0; even at €120,000 in class I (tax €31,127) soli is only €1,282.46 a year, not the full 5.5%.

Why is my health contribution not simply 14.6% of my salary?

Because 14.6% is the combined employer-plus-employee rate. You pay 7.3%, plus half of your insurer's supplementary contribution (Zusatzbeitrag) — at the 2026 average of 2.9% that makes your share 8.75%. Your employer pays the other half, and both parts stop at the €69,750 ceiling.

Does this calculator include my employer's contributions?

No. It shows your payslip: only the employee shares are deducted from your gross. Your employer pays roughly the same again on top — their halves of the pension, unemployment, health and care contributions, plus accident insurance in full. That is employer cost, never part of your gross, and it does not appear in your net.

Why does an extra euro of gross lose so much value in the middle income range?

Between €17,800 and €69,878 of taxable income you are in the second progressive zone of the §32a tariff, where the marginal rate climbs steadily from about 24% to 42% — the formula is a curve, not steps. Stacked on roughly 21% of social contributions, each extra euro of gross can lose close to half its value before any church tax. The annual return, not the payslip, settles the final figure.

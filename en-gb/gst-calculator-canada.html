How it is calculated

Sales tax in Canada is the federal 5% GST plus a provincial layer that depends on the province of supply. The two formulas are:

  • Add GST/HST: GST/HST = Net × 13 ÷ 100, and Gross = Net + GST/HST.
  • Remove GST/HST: Net = Gross ÷ (1 + 13 ÷ 100), and GST/HST = Gross − Net.
  • Standard rate: 13%. Other rates: 5% GST alone, 14% or 15% HST, and the PST/RST/QST combinations — all in the rate selector above.

Note on combined rates: in BC, Saskatchewan, Manitoba and Quebec the provincial tax is legally separate from GST. In Quebec, QST is calculated on the GST-exclusive price (it was harmonised in 2013), so the simple combined-rate maths on this page is exact for totals. Small differences can arise on items taxed by one level but not the other.

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard GST/HST rate in Canada?

The standard GST/HST rate in Canada is 13%. Canada does not have a single rate: the federal GST is 5% everywhere, and provinces either harmonise it into HST or add their own provincial tax. This page defaults to Ontario's 13% HST — pick your province in the rate selector. GST/HST is called Goods and Services Tax / Harmonized Sales Tax (GST/HST) locally, amounts on this page are in Canadian dollars (CAD), and the rates here were verified in November 2026 against Canada Revenue Agency (GST/HST rates) and Revenu Québec / provincial finance ministries (QST, PST, RST).

Which provinces charge HST, and at what rates?

Ontario charges 13% HST (5% federal + 8% provincial). New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, and Prince Edward Island charge 15% HST (5% + 10%). Nova Scotia reduced its HST to 14% (5% + 9%) on 1 April 2025. In the other provinces the provincial tax (PST in BC and Saskatchewan, RST in Manitoba, QST in Quebec) is a separate tax administered separately, even though the combined consumer total is often what matters for price calculations.

How do I add GST/HST to a price in Canada?

Multiply the net price by 0.13 to get the GST/HST amount at the 13% standard rate, then add it to the net price — or use the Add GST/HST mode above. For example, on a net price of $ 100.00 the GST/HST is $ 13.00 and the gross price is $ 113.00.

How do I remove GST/HST from a gross price in Canada?

Divide the gross price by 1.13 to get the net amount at the 13% standard rate, then subtract the net from the gross to get the GST/HST — or use the Remove GST/HST mode above. For example, a gross price of $ 100.00 contains $ 11.50 of GST/HST, leaving a net amount of $ 88.50.

Are PST and QST exactly the same as HST?

No — and it matters for businesses. HST is a value-added tax like GST: businesses claim input tax credits. PST/RST are retail sales taxes with narrower credit rules, and QST is a value-added tax administered by Revenu Québec with its own registration. They also differ in exactly which goods and services are taxed (for example, BC PST applies to some services that GST does not). For a consumer price total, the combined rates in the selector are the right figures; for filing, each tax is reported separately.

How it works — the formula

The calculator stores no data and does one piece of arithmetic per mode. In Add GST/HST mode it treats your amount as the net price: it multiplies the net by the chosen rate divided by 100 to get the GST/HST, then adds the two together for the gross price you charge. In Remove GST/HST mode it treats your amount as a gross, GST/HST-inclusive price — the kind printed on a Canada receipt or shelf label — and divides it by (1 + rate ÷ 100) to recover the net amount; the difference between the gross and the net is the GST/HST contained in the price. Because consumer prices in Canada are usually shown before tax at the till in many provinces — while posted HST-inclusive pricing is common in others — the two modes let you work either way: removing tax from a gross figure and then adding it back returns the original amount, apart from rounding to two decimal places in CAD.

Frequently asked questions

What must a sales invoice in Canada show?

An invoice for GST/HST purposes should show the seller's name and GST/HST account number, the date, a description of the goods or services, the amount paid, and the tax paid or a statement that tax is included — plus the buyer's name for larger sales. In PST/QST provinces the provincial tax and registration numbers are shown as separate lines.

I sell from Canada to customers in other provinces or countries — which rate applies?

Generally the rate of the province where the goods are delivered or the services are consumed ('place of supply' rules), not your own province — so an Ontario seller shipping to BC charges 5% GST plus BC PST rules, and goods exported outside Canada are usually zero-rated for GST/HST. Registration duties can arise in PST provinces and Quebec even for out-of-province sellers.

Are consumer prices in Canada shown including tax?

Often not: in most provinces shelf prices exclude tax, which is added at the till (fuel and some items differ). Where a price is quoted tax-included, use the Remove mode to split out the net and the tax at your province's rate.

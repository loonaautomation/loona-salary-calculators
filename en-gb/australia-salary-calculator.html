How it is calculated

  • Resident tax rates 2025–26: 0% to $18,200; 16% to $45,000; 30% to $135,000; 37% to $190,000; 45% above.
  • LITO (low income tax offset): up to $700, phasing out between $37,500 and $66,667 of income — subtracted from the tax after the scale is applied.
  • Medicare levy: 2% of taxable income. Below about $27,222 no levy is payable; between roughly $27,222 and $34,027 it shades in at 10 cents per dollar, so you never pay more than the full 2% (single thresholds for 2025–26).
  • Superannuation: your employer pays the 12% super guarantee on top of the salary entered here (when the salary is quoted as base pay). If your package is quoted as "including super", subtract the super first, then use this calculator.

Worked example: on $80,000 — scale tax $14,788, no LITO (income above $66,667), Medicare levy $1,600 → net $63,612 a year, $5,301 a month. The live example above uses $80,000.00: tax $14,788.00, Medicare $1,600.00, net $63,612.00.

Frequently asked questions

What are the Australian tax rates for 2025–26?

For residents: no tax on the first $18,200, then 16% to $45,000, 30% to $135,000, 37% to $190,000 and 45% above that, plus the 2% Medicare levy and minus the low income tax offset (up to $700) at lower incomes. From 1 July 2026 the 16% rate falls to 15% for the 2026–27 year — this page uses the 2025–26 rates as labelled.

Is superannuation deducted from my salary?

No. The super guarantee (12% of ordinary time earnings in 2025–26) is paid by your employer into your super fund on top of your wages when your salary is quoted exclusive of super. It only reduces take-home if your contract quotes a total package including super.

What is the Medicare levy and who is exempt?

A 2% levy on taxable income that funds Medicare. Singles earning under about $27,222 in 2025–26 pay none, with a shade-in range up to about $34,027. Some people (certain pensioners, foreign residents, those without Medicare eligibility) are exempt or pay half — this calculator applies the standard single rate.

How much tax do I pay on $100,000 in Australia?

On the 2025–26 resident scale: $20,788 of income tax plus $2,000 Medicare levy, leaving $77,212 a year — about $6,434 a month. Try it above: the calculator shows the same figures live.

Do non-residents use the same rates?

No. Foreign residents for tax purposes get no $18,200 tax-free threshold and pay higher rates from the first dollar (30% on the first band in 2025–26), and usually no Medicare levy. This calculator is for Australian tax residents.

How it works — the formula

Net = gross − income tax − Medicare levy. Income tax is computed on the five-band resident scale (0% to $18,200; 16% to $45,000; 30% to $135,000; 37% to $190,000; 45% above), then the low income tax offset is subtracted — $700 at incomes up to $37,500, phasing out at 5 cents per dollar to $45,000 and 1.5 cents per dollar to $66,667. The Medicare levy is 2% of income, with a low-income exemption below about $27,222 and a 10% shade-in up to about $34,027 so the levy never exceeds 2% of income.

Worked example

Gross $80,000.00, resident, 2025–26. Scale tax on $80,000.00 comes to $14,788.00 after the low income tax offset (which is $0 at this income — it phases out by $66,667). Medicare levy = 2% × $80,000.00 = $1,600.00. Net = $63,612.00 a year — $5,301.00 a month. The effective total deduction rate is $20.48% of gross.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my employer withhold more than this early in the year?

PAYG withholding uses ATO tax tables per pay period, so irregular pay (overtime, bonuses, back pay) is withheld as if that period's pay continued all year. The annual return squares it up — over-withholding comes back as a refund.

Does this include a HELP or HECS student debt?

No. Compulsory HELP repayments are collected through payroll once income passes the threshold (around $54,000 in 2025–26) at rates rising with income. If you have a study debt, your real net will be lower than shown here.

What changes on 1 July 2026?

The 16% bracket rate drops to 15% (and to 14% from 1 July 2027) under the legislated extra tax cuts, worth up to a few hundred dollars a year for most workers. Thresholds and the Medicare levy settings are unchanged.

Are casual loading and overtime treated differently?

No — they are ordinary income taxed on the same scale. The only difference is practical: withholding on a big fortnight can look heavy, but the annual calculation is identical.

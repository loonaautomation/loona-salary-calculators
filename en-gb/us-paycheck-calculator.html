Frequently asked questions

How is take-home pay calculated?

Gross salary minus federal income tax (progressive brackets on taxable income after the standard deduction), minus Social Security (6.2% up to $184,500) and Medicare (1.45%, plus 0.9% on high wages), minus state income tax where it applies.

Which states have no income tax?

Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas and Wyoming levy no individual income tax; Washington taxes capital gains only, not wages.

What is the 2026 standard deduction?

$16,100 for single filers, $32,200 for married filing jointly, $24,150 for heads of household (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32).

Does this include local taxes?

No — city and county income taxes (New York City, Maryland counties and others) are additional and are not included.

How it works — the formula

Federal tax is computed on taxable income (gross minus the standard deduction) using the 2026 IRS brackets, applied progressively — each band of income is taxed at its own rate. Social Security is 6.2% of wages up to the 2026 wage base; Medicare is 1.45% of all wages, plus 0.9% on wages above $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married filing jointly). Net pay is gross minus federal tax, Social Security, Medicare and state tax. This is an estimate for a single earner with no other income, no dependents and no itemised deductions — your actual pay may differ.

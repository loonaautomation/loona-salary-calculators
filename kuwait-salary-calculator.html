Kuwait Net Salary Calculator

Kuwait charges employees no income tax at all. Expatriates take home their full gross salary; Kuwaiti citizens contribute to the PIFSS social security fund (10.5% employee share).

In short: Kuwait has no income tax for employees — on a KWD 18,000.00 gross salary an expatriate nets the full KWD 18,000.00. Kuwaiti citizens pay a 10.5% PIFSS contribution toward their pension. The other end-of-job amount, the indemnity, is calculated separately.

How it is calculated

  • Income tax: Kuwait levies no personal income tax on anyone's salary. (Its 15% corporate tax applies only to foreign companies' Kuwait profits — never to an employee's payslip.)
  • PIFSS (citizens only): Kuwaiti employees contribute to the Public Institution for Social Security. The employee share is 10.5% of contributory salary (the 7.5% basic share plus additional statutory contributions); the employer adds 11.5%. Contributions are charged on the contributory salary, which is capped, so on very high salaries the effective percentage falls. Expatriates are not members and pay nothing.
  • End-of-service indemnity: expatriates earn an indemnity instead of a pension — for monthly-paid staff, 15 days' salary per year for the first five years and one month per year after that, under the Kuwait Labour Law. It is paid at the end of the job, not deducted monthly: use the Kuwait Indemnity Calculator linked below.

Worked example: a Kuwaiti citizen on KWD 18,000.00 gross pays 10.5% PIFSS = KWD 1,890 and nets KWD 16,110 a year. An expatriate on the same salary nets the full KWD 18,000.00.

Frequently asked questions

Is there income tax in Kuwait?

No. Kuwait has no personal income tax — salaries are paid in full for expatriates and citizens alike. The only standard payslip deduction is the PIFSS social security contribution, and it applies to Kuwaiti citizens only.

What is PIFSS and who pays it?

PIFSS is the Public Institution for Social Security, Kuwait's pension fund. Kuwaiti employees contribute an employee share of 10.5% of contributory salary (7.5% basic plus additional statutory contributions) and employers add 11.5%. Expatriate employees are not covered and contribute nothing.

What is the Kuwait end-of-service indemnity?

It is the lump sum expatriates (and resigning citizens outside PIFSS rules) receive when employment ends. For monthly-paid employees it accrues at 15 days of salary per year for the first five years and one month of salary per year after that, calculated on the last salary. Work yours out with our Kuwait Indemnity Calculator.

Is the PIFSS contribution charged on my whole salary?

It is charged on the contributory salary as defined by PIFSS, which is capped — so the 10.5% in this calculator is exact for most salaries and slightly overstates the deduction at very high pay. Your employer's PIFSS statement shows the exact contributory figure.

Are bonuses and allowances taxed in Kuwait?

No. With no income tax, every element of your package is paid in full. Note that the end-of-service indemnity is calculated on your total last salary for monthly-paid staff, unlike some Gulf states that use basic salary only.

How it works — the formula

Net = gross − income tax − social security. In Kuwait the income tax term is zero for everyone, and the social security term is zero for expatriates, so for most of the workforce net = gross. For a Kuwaiti citizen, net = gross × (1 − 0.105) = gross × 0.895 at the 10.5% PIFSS employee share, subject to the contributory-salary cap.

Worked example

Expatriate, gross KWD 18,000.00 a year. Income tax: KWD 0. PIFSS: KWD 0 (expatriates are not members). Net = KWD 18,000.00 a year — KWD 1,500.00 a month. A Kuwaiti citizen on the same salary pays 10.5% × KWD 18,000.00 = KWD 1,890 into PIFSS and nets KWD 16,110 a year (KWD 1,342.50 a month).

Frequently asked questions

Why do citizens accept a lower monthly net than expatriates?

Because the PIFSS contribution buys a state pension and social insurance cover for life. Expatriates get no pension from Kuwait — their retirement provision from a Kuwait job is the end-of-service indemnity alone.

Does my employer pay anything on top of my salary?

For Kuwaiti staff the employer adds 11.5% PIFSS on top of salary. For expatriates there is no equivalent statutory charge — the employer's cost is the salary itself.

Is the indemnity reduced if I resign?

It can be. For monthly-paid employees who resign, the indemnity is scaled by years of service (for example half between three and five years, in the standard cases set by the Labour Law). The Kuwait Indemnity Calculator applies the resignation scale.

Are there any other deductions in Kuwait?

Only ones you agree to — loan repayments, for example. There is no income tax, no expatriate social insurance and no unemployment contribution deducted from salaries in Kuwait.

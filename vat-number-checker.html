How it is calculated

There is no arithmetic here — VAT numbers are checked against a published pattern for each country:

  • Normalise: spaces, dots and dashes are removed and the number is converted to uppercase; the matching two-letter country prefix is stripped where a country uses one (Switzerland keeps its CHE prefix; Norway's MVA suffix and Switzerland's TVA/IVA/MWST suffix are removed).
  • Pattern test: the remaining characters must match the country's format exactly — for example Germany is nine digits, France is two letters or digits followed by nine digits, the Netherlands is nine digits, the letter B, then two digits, and Malta is six digits followed by two letters.
  • Verdict: the result states clearly whether the format looks valid for the selected country, and reminds you that format is not registration.

Frequently asked questions

Does this checker confirm that a VAT number is registered?

No. This checker validates format only — whether the number follows the official pattern for its country. It cannot confirm that the number is registered, active, or belongs to a particular business. For registration, use the official EU VIES service, or the UK government checker for UK numbers.

What is VIES and when should I use it?

VIES (VAT Information Exchange System) is the European Commission's free official service for checking whether an EU VAT number is registered for cross-border trade. Use it before zero-rating an EU business customer. This page links directly to VIES below the checker.

Why did a real VAT number fail this format check?

Most often because of typing: extra letters, a missing country prefix where one is required, or the wrong number of digits. Check the pattern shown for the country, remove spaces and punctuation, and try again. If the format is correct but you need certainty about registration, check VIES.

Do I enter the country letters at the start of the number?

Either way works here. This checker strips the two-letter country prefix automatically when it matches the country you selected, so DE123456789 and 123456789 are both accepted for Germany. Switzerland is the exception: Swiss numbers keep their CHE prefix.

How do I check a UK VAT number's registration?

Format and registration are separate in the UK too. After this format check, use the official UK government service — Check a UK VAT number — linked below the checker, which confirms whether a UK number is registered and shows the business name.

How it works — the formula

Every European tax authority publishes the exact shape its VAT numbers must take: how many characters, which positions are digits and which are letters, and in a few cases fixed letters such as the B in Dutch numbers or the U at the start of Austrian numbers. A format check is therefore a pattern match, not a calculation: normalise the input, strip the country prefix, and test what remains against that country's pattern. Passing proves only that the number could exist in that shape — a mistyped digit inside a correctly shaped number, or a correctly shaped number that was never issued, can only be caught by an official registration database such as VIES.

Worked example

Take the German example DE123456789. Normalising removes nothing except the DE prefix, leaving … — nine digits, which is exactly the German pattern, so the verdict is that the format looks valid for Germany. The same number shortened to DE123 leaves only three digits and fails, as the second worked example above shows. Neither result says anything about registration: that final step always belongs to VIES (or the UK checker for a GB number).

Frequently asked questions

What format does a German VAT number have?

A German VAT number (USt-IdNr.) is exactly nine digits, for example DE123456789 after its DE prefix. It is different from the domestic German tax number (Steuernummer), which has a different format and must not be used for EU cross-border trade.

Why do some countries allow more than one VAT format?

A few countries issue more than one pattern — Ireland and Spain each have older and newer patterns, and Lithuania issues 9-digit numbers to legal persons and 12-digit numbers to temporary registrations. This checker accepts every published pattern for the selected country.

Can I rely on a format check for invoicing?

A format check is a useful first screen against typos, but for zero-rated EU supplies you should keep evidence of a VIES registration check with its consultation number, alongside the invoice. Format alone is not evidence that your customer is VAT-registered.

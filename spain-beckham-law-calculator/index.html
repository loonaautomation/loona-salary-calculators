Beckham Law Calculator — 24% Flat vs Standard IRPF

Spain's special expat regime taxes employment income at a flat 24% instead of the progressive scale. But it is not always the winner — compare both on your salary, including the full six-year saving.

In short: on a €100,000 salary, the Beckham regime's tax is €24,000 against €32,156.48 on the standard scale — a saving of €8,156.48 a year, or €48,938.87 over the six-year regime. Below roughly €55,000–€60,000 the standard scale usually wins, because Beckham gives no personal minimum.

How it is calculated

  • Beckham tax = 24% × employment income up to €600,000 + 47% × any excess. The regime's tax is charged on the employment income itself: there is no personal minimum and no €2,000 expenses deduction under the special regime.
  • Standard tax = our Spain Net Salary method: IRPF scale on (gross − social security − €2,000), minus the tax on the €5,550 personal minimum.
  • Social security (6.50%, capped base €61,214.40) is identical under both regimes and is deducted in both columns.
  • Six-year figure = the one-year saving × 6 (the year of arrival plus five following years), assuming the same salary throughout.
  • The standard column uses the standard (common-territory) IRPF scale; in a high-tax autonomous community the standard column would be worse and the Beckham saving larger.

Worked example: gross €100,000 → Beckham tax €24,000, net €72,021.06; standard tax €32,156.48, net €63,864.59. Saving €8,156.48 a year; €48,938.87 over six years. Counter-example: on €30,000 the Beckham tax of €7,200 is more than the standard €4,926 — the regime would cost you €2,274 a year.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Beckham law?

A special regime (Article 93 LIRPF) for people moving to Spain to work: flat 24% on employment income up to €600,000 (47% above), for the arrival year plus five more years.

Who qualifies?

A qualifying work-related move, no Spanish tax residence in the previous five tax years, and an application within six months of Social Security registration (Modelo 149). Spouse and children under 25 can opt in too.

Is it always better?

No — under roughly €55,000–€60,000 the standard scale with its personal minimum is often cheaper. The table above tells you which side you are on.

Do I still pay social security?

Yes, in full, under both regimes. Only the income tax changes.

What is not covered at 24%?

Savings income is taxed at the normal 19–30% savings rates, and foreign-source non-employment income is generally outside Spanish tax under the regime.

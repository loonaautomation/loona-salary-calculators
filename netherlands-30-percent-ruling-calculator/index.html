Netherlands 30% Ruling Calculator

What is the expat ruling actually worth on your salary? Compare net pay with and without it — including the salary-norm limit most calculators skip, and a preview of the 2027 drop to 27%.

In short: the 30% ruling lets your employer pay up to 30% of your salary tax-free, but your remaining taxable salary must stay above the 2026 norm of €48,013 (€36,497 for under-30s with a master's), and the allowance only applies to the first €262,000. On €100,000 the ruling is worth about €16,336 a year; from 2027 it drops to 27% for most holders.

How it is calculated

  • The same verified 2026 Box 1 engine as our Netherlands Salary Calculator runs your salary three times: no ruling, 30% ruling, and the 2027 rate.
  • Allowance: 30% of salary (applied only up to the €262,000 WNT cap, so at most €78,600), then limited by the salary norm — taxable salary after the allowance must exceed €48,013 (or €36,497 under-30 master's). If your salary cannot clear the norm even with a reduced allowance, the ruling gives nothing.
  • 2027 preview: from 1 January 2027 the rate falls to 27% for rulings first applied from 2024; pre-2024 rulings keep 30%. The preview uses the 27% rate with the 2026 salary norms, because the final 2027 norms had not been published by the Belastingdienst at the time of writing — treat the 2027 column as an indication, not a promise.
  • Tax credits (general and labour) are calculated on the reduced taxable salary, exactly as payroll does — which is why the benefit is a little less than a naive "30% × your tax rate" guess.

Worked example: gross €100,000 → without the ruling, net €62,710.89. With the 30% ruling: allowance €30,000, tax €20,953.27, net €79,046.73 — a benefit of €16,335.84 a year. At the 2027 rate of 27% the same salary nets €77,532.69.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the 30% ruling worth?

On €60,000 about €6,050 a year (the norm limits the allowance there); on €100,000 about €16,336 a year. Your figure is in the table above.

Why is my allowance smaller than 30%?

The salary norm: taxable salary after the allowance must stay above €48,013 (2026). On €60,000 the allowance is therefore cut to €11,987. The €262,000 salary cap also limits very high earners.

What changes in 2027?

The rate drops to 27% for rulings first applied from 2024; pre-2024 rulings keep 30% for their remaining term. The 2027 preview uses 2026 norms, as the final 2027 norms are not yet published.

How long does the ruling last?

Up to five years, reduced by any earlier periods of living or working in the Netherlands.

Does the ruling change my gross salary?

No — the gross stays the same; up to 30% of it is simply paid tax-free under a written agreement with your employer.

How it works — the formula

The same 2026 Box 1 engine as our Netherlands salary calculator runs your salary three times: without the ruling, with the 30% ruling, and at the 2027 rate. The allowance is 30% of salary (27% in the 2027 preview), applied only to the first €262,000 of salary, and then limited so that your taxable salary after the allowance still exceeds the salary norm — €48,013, or €36,497 if you are under 30 with a qualifying master's degree. If the salary cannot clear the norm even with a reduced allowance, the ruling gives nothing. The tax credits are calculated on the reduced taxable salary, exactly as payroll does — which is why the benefit is the tax difference between the columns, not 30% of your salary or 30% of your tax.

Worked example

Gross €75,000, holiday allowance included, no pension contribution, standard norm, ruling first applied from 2024. Without the ruling, tax is €23,476.67 and net pay is €51,523.33 a year. With the 30% ruling, the allowance is the full €22,500 — the remaining €52,500 of taxable salary clears the norm — so tax falls to €12,121.37 and net rises to €62,878.63: a benefit of €11,355.30 a year. At the 2027 preview rate of 27%, the allowance is €20,250 and the benefit is €10,219.77.

Frequently asked questions

Can the ruling be applied to only part of my salary, such as a bonus?

The ruling works through payroll on your salary as a whole: up to the ruling percentage of your total salary from the Dutch employer is paid tax-free, rather than being allocated component by component. This calculator therefore applies the percentage to the full gross you enter (up to the €262,000 cap) — enter your total Dutch employment income, including bonuses from the same employer.

What happens to the ruling if I change employer?

It is not lost automatically: you and the new employer can apply to continue the ruling, with a fresh written agreement, and the new employer must be a Dutch wage-tax withholding agent. The five-year clock keeps running from the original start date, so only the unused part of the term carries over.

Why does the 2027 column use the 2026 salary norms?

Because the Belastingdienst had not published the final 2027 norms when this calculator was built. The 2027 column pairs the known 27% rate with the 2026 norms (€48,013 / €36,497) and is labelled a preview — the real 2027 norms are expected to be higher, which would make the preview slightly optimistic for borderline salaries.

My salary is €300,000 — why does the allowance stop at €78,600?

The ruling percentage applies only to salary up to the WNT (Balkenende) cap of €262,000 in 2026: 30% × €262,000 = €78,600, however high the actual salary is. The calculator applies that cap in all three columns.

Is the benefit the same in every year of the ruling?

Not exactly. The percentage is fixed for your term — with the 2027 drop for rulings first applied from 2024 — but the euro benefit moves with your salary and with each year's brackets, credits and salary norm, which are all indexed annually. Re-run the calculator each January with the new salary to see the current benefit.

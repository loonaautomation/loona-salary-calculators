How it is calculated

GST in New Zealand is charged at 15% on most supplies. The two formulas are:

  • Add GST: GST = Net × 15 ÷ 100, and Gross = Net + GST.
  • Remove GST: Net = Gross ÷ (1 + 15 ÷ 100), and GST = Gross − Net.
  • Standard rate: 15%. Other rates: a single flat rate of 15% — no reduced rate; zero-rated and exempt categories apply instead (see the FAQ).

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard GST rate in New Zealand?

The standard GST rate in New Zealand is 15%. It applies to nearly all goods and services supplied in New Zealand. The rate has been 15% since 1 October 2010 (previously 12.5%). GST is called Goods and Services Tax (GST) locally, amounts on this page are in New Zealand dollars (NZD), and the rates here were verified in November 2026 against Inland Revenue (Te Tari Taake) — GST rate and guidance.

Are there reduced or zero GST rates in New Zealand?

There is no reduced rate — New Zealand famously keeps a single rate with very few exceptions. Zero-rated (0%) supplies include exports, the sale of a business as a going concern, and land sold between GST-registered parties when the conditions are met. Exempt supplies are mainly financial services, residential rent and donated goods sold by charities.

How do I add GST to a price in New Zealand?

Multiply the net price by 0.15 to get the GST amount at the 15% standard rate, then add it to the net price — or use the Add GST mode above. For example, on a net price of NZ$ 100.00 the GST is NZ$ 15.00 and the gross price is NZ$ 115.00.

How do I remove GST from a gross price in New Zealand?

Divide the gross price by 1.15 to get the net amount at the 15% standard rate, then subtract the net from the gross to get the GST — or use the Remove GST mode above. For example, a gross price of NZ$ 100.00 contains NZ$ 13.04 of GST, leaving a net amount of NZ$ 86.96.

How do I extract the GST from a New Zealand price quickly?

Multiply the GST-inclusive price by 3 and divide by 23. Because GST is 15% of the net price, the GST fraction of a gross price is 15/115 = 3/23: a NZ$115.00 price contains NZ$15.00 of GST. The Remove GST mode above does the same for any amount.

How it works — the formula

The calculator stores no data and does one piece of arithmetic per mode. In Add GST mode it treats your amount as the net price: it multiplies the net by the chosen rate divided by 100 to get the GST, then adds the two together for the gross price you charge. In Remove GST mode it treats your amount as a gross, GST-inclusive price — the kind printed on a New Zealand receipt or shelf label — and divides it by (1 + rate ÷ 100) to recover the net amount; the difference between the gross and the net is the GST contained in the price. Because consumer prices in New Zealand are displayed GST-inclusive and business prices are often quoted plus GST, the two modes are exact inverses: removing GST from a gross figure and then adding it back returns the original amount, apart from rounding to two decimal places in NZD.

Frequently asked questions

What must a tax invoice in New Zealand show?

A tax invoice must show the words 'tax invoice', the seller's name and GST number, the date, a description of the goods or services, and the GST amount or a statement that the price includes GST. Since April 2023 the rules are information-based rather than format-based, so compliant records (including e-invoices) count.

I sell from New Zealand to customers overseas — is GST charged?

Exports are zero-rated: you charge 0% GST and can still claim GST on your costs, as long as the export rules and evidence requirements are met. Services supplied to non-residents who are outside New Zealand when the services are performed can also be zero-rated.

Are consumer prices in New Zealand shown including GST?

Yes. Prices for consumers are displayed GST-inclusive, so a shelf price is a gross price — use the Remove GST mode to split out the net and the GST. Always check whether a business quote says 'plus GST' (net) or 'incl. GST' (gross) before paying.

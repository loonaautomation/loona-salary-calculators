Kuwait Indemnity Calculator

End-of-service indemnity for monthly-paid employees under Articles 51–53 of the Kuwait Labour Law (Law No. 6 of 2010), including the resignation scale and the 18-month cap — with the calculation method stated openly, because Kuwait practice is not uniform.

In short: Kuwait indemnity is 15 days' wage per year for the first five years (daily wage = monthly wage ÷ 26) and one full month's wage per year after that, capped at 18 months' wage. If you resign on an indefinite contract, you receive nothing under 3 years, half at 3–5 years, two thirds at 5–10 years, and the full amount at 10+ years.

How it is calculated — and the method disclosure

  • First 5 years: 15 days' wage per year. Kuwait practice converts a monthly wage to a daily wage by dividing by 26 (working days), so this part = years × 15 × (wage ÷ 26).
  • After 5 years: Article 51 says "one month's remuneration" per year. Method used here: one full monthly wage per year — the method most calculators use. A minority compute 30 × (wage ÷ 26) instead, which is roughly 15% higher for those years. There is no published ruling settling the point, so this page states its method rather than hiding it.
  • Wage base: Article 55 points to the full wage, and this calculator uses your last full monthly wage. Some practitioners argue basic-only; that question is likewise unsettled, so if your employer uses basic salary, your figure will be lower.
  • Cap: total indemnity cannot exceed 1.5 years' wage (18 months).
  • Resignation (Article 53, indefinite contracts): under 3 years = 0; 3 to <5 years = ½; 5 to <10 years = ⅔; 10+ years = full. A fixed-term contract that runs to expiry pays in full.
  • Part-years are pro-rated. Daily/weekly/hourly-paid workers have a different scale (10 days, then 15 days per year, cap 1 year) — not covered by this calculator.

Worked example: wage KWD 1,000, 7 years, employer-ended → first 5 years: 5 × 15 × (1,000 ÷ 26) = KWD 2,884.62; next 2 years: 2 × 1,000 = KWD 2,000; total KWD 4,884.62. The same service resigned at 7 years pays two thirds: KWD 3,256.41.

How it works — the formula

For monthly-paid employees, Articles 51 to 53 of the Kuwait Labour Law (Law No. 6 of 2010) build the indemnity in two parts, then apply the resignation share and the cap. In formula form:

  • Daily wage = last monthly wage ÷ 26
  • First 5 years = (years of service, up to 5) × 15 × daily wage
  • Each year after the fifth = one full monthly wage per year
  • If you resigned (indefinite contract): multiply the total by your Article 53 share — 0 under 3 years, ½ at 3 to under 5 years, ⅔ at 5 to under 10 years, the full amount at 10 years or more
  • Cap: the total can never exceed 18 × monthly wage

The wage used throughout is your last full monthly wage (Article 55), and part-years are pro-rated.

Worked example

Monthly wage KWD 600, 8 years of service, and the employer ends the contract. Daily wage = 600 ÷ 26 ≈ KWD 23.0769. The first 5 years earn 15 days per year, i.e. 75 days: 75 × 23.0769 = KWD 1,730.77. Years 6 to 8: 3 × 600 = KWD 1,800. Total = 1,730.77 + 1,800 = KWD 3,530.77, well below the cap of 18 × 600 = KWD 10,800. If the same employee had resigned instead, 8 years falls in the 5 to under 10 band, so two thirds is payable: 3,530.77 × ⅔ = KWD 2,353.85.

Frequently asked questions

Who is entitled to Kuwait indemnity?

Employees covered by Labour Law 6/2010 earn indemnity for their service; how much they receive depends on how the contract ends. This calculator covers monthly-paid employees, who form the majority of the private-sector workforce.

Are hourly- or daily-paid workers calculated the same way?

No. Workers paid by the hour, day or week follow a different scale under the same law: 10 days’ wage per year for the first five years, then 15 days per year, with a cap of one year’s wage. That scale is not covered by this calculator, which is built for monthly-paid staff.

Are part-years counted, or only full years?

Part-years are pro-rated. Service is measured from your joining date to your last working day, and a fraction of a year earns the same fraction of that year’s indemnity — this calculator works from your exact dates.

My fixed-term contract simply ended. Do I get the full amount?

Yes. When a fixed-term contract runs to its expiry, the full indemnity is payable — the Article 53 resignation reductions apply to resignations on indefinite contracts, not to a contract that ends on its agreed date.

Why does the calculator divide the monthly wage by 26?

That is the Kuwait practice for turning a monthly wage into a daily wage: a month is counted as 26 working days. It is used for the first five years, where the law grants 15 days per year. For years after the fifth, this calculator uses one full monthly wage per year instead — the method most calculators use, disclosed openly above.

Is the indemnity based on my basic salary or my full salary?

This calculator uses your last full monthly wage, because Article 55 defines the wage broadly. Some practitioners argue for basic salary only, and no published ruling settles the question — if your employer calculates on basic salary alone, your figure will be lower than the estimate here.

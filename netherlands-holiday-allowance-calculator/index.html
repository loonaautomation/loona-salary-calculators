Netherlands Holiday Allowance Calculator

Your vakantiegeld: the statutory 8% of gross salary that lands (usually) every May — and the surprisingly large slice of tax that comes off it before it reaches you.

In short: Dutch holiday allowance is at least 8% of your gross annual salary. On a €50,000 salary quoted excluding the allowance, that is €4,000 gross — but because it is taxed at the special rate, roughly €2,019 comes off in tax at 2026 rates, leaving about €1,981 net. The exact tax depends on your total income and tax credits.

How it is calculated

  • The allowance: at least 8% of gross annual salary (Minimum Wage and Minimum Holiday Allowance Act). Salary quoted excluding it: allowance = 8% × salary. Salary including it: allowance = salary − salary ÷ 1.08.
  • The tax: the allowance is taxed as a special-rate (bijzonder tarief) payment. This calculator runs the verified 2026 Box 1 engine twice — tax on your total income with the allowance, minus tax without it — so the figure includes the top-bracket effect and the phase-out of your general and labour tax credits. That is the honest reason the rate looks so high.
  • Payroll withholding in May can differ slightly from the annual figure; over-withholding comes back through your annual tax return.
  • With the 30% ruling, the allowance is part of the salary the ruling applies to (subject to the salary norm), which lowers the tax on it.

Worked example: salary €50,000 excluding holiday allowance → allowance €4,000; tax on it €2,018.72 (an effective 50.47%, because the €4,000 lands in the credit phase-out zone); net allowance €1,981.28.

Frequently asked questions

How much holiday allowance do you get?

At least 8% of gross annual salary, by law. €4,000 on a €50,000 salary that excludes it; the 8/108 share if your salary already includes it.

Why is so much tax taken off it?

It is taxed as a special-rate payment: the whole amount lands in your top bracket and reduces your tax credits, so effective rates of 40–50% on the allowance are normal. Over-withholding is corrected in your annual return.

When is it paid?

Usually in May, for June-to-May accrual. Some employers pay monthly instead; leavers receive the accrued part in their final settlement.

Does the 30% ruling apply to it?

Yes — the allowance is part of the salary covered by the ruling, subject to the salary norm. Select the ruling option above to see the effect.

Is it on top of my salary or included?

Both exist. Always ask which an offer means — on €50,000 the difference is €4,000 a year.

Netherlands Holiday Allowance Calculator

Your vakantiegeld: the statutory 8% of gross salary that lands (usually) every May — and the surprisingly large slice of tax that comes off it before it reaches you.

In short: Dutch holiday allowance is at least 8% of your gross annual salary. On a €50,000 salary quoted excluding the allowance, that is €4,000 gross — but because it is taxed at the special rate, roughly €2,019 comes off in tax at 2026 rates, leaving about €1,981 net. The exact tax depends on your total income and tax credits.

How it is calculated

  • The allowance: at least 8% of gross annual salary (Minimum Wage and Minimum Holiday Allowance Act). Salary quoted excluding it: allowance = 8% × salary. Salary including it: allowance = salary − salary ÷ 1.08.
  • The tax: the allowance is taxed as a special-rate (bijzonder tarief) payment. This calculator runs the verified 2026 Box 1 engine twice — tax on your total income with the allowance, minus tax without it — so the figure includes the top-bracket effect and the phase-out of your general and labour tax credits. That is the honest reason the rate looks so high.
  • Payroll withholding in May can differ slightly from the annual figure; over-withholding comes back through your annual tax return.
  • With the 30% ruling, the allowance is part of the salary the ruling applies to (subject to the salary norm), which lowers the tax on it.

Worked example: salary €50,000 excluding holiday allowance → allowance €4,000; tax on it €2,018.72 (an effective 50.47%, because the €4,000 lands in the credit phase-out zone); net allowance €1,981.28.

Frequently asked questions

How much holiday allowance do you get?

At least 8% of gross annual salary, by law. €4,000 on a €50,000 salary that excludes it; the 8/108 share if your salary already includes it.

Why is so much tax taken off it?

It is taxed as a special-rate payment: the whole amount lands in your top bracket and reduces your tax credits, so effective rates of 40–50% on the allowance are normal. Over-withholding is corrected in your annual return.

When is it paid?

Usually in May, for June-to-May accrual. Some employers pay monthly instead; leavers receive the accrued part in their final settlement.

Does the 30% ruling apply to it?

Yes — the allowance is part of the salary covered by the ruling, subject to the salary norm. Select the ruling option above to see the effect.

Is it on top of my salary or included?

Both exist. Always ask which an offer means — on €50,000 the difference is €4,000 a year.

How it works — the formula

The allowance itself is simple: at least 8% of gross annual salary. If your salary excludes it, allowance = 8% × salary; if the salary already includes it, allowance = salary − salary ÷ 1.08. The tax on it is not a flat rate: this calculator runs the verified 2026 Box 1 engine on your total income twice — with the allowance and without it — and takes the difference. That is exactly how the special rate (bijzonder tarief) behaves, because the allowance stacks on top of your other income at your highest bracket and also eats into the phase-out of your general and labour tax credits.

Worked example

Gross salary €65,000 excluding holiday allowance, no 30% ruling. Allowance: 8% × €65,000 = €5,200, taking total gross to €70,200. Tax on the allowance: €2,624.34 — an effective rate of 50.47%, higher than the 49.50% top bracket, because the extra €5,200 also reduces the tax credits. Net holiday allowance: €5,200 − €2,624.34 = €2,575.66.

Frequently asked questions

I started my job mid-year — do I still get the full 8%?

The allowance builds up over the months you actually work (in most contracts the accrual year runs from June to May). Start halfway through the accrual year and you receive roughly half the annual amount at the next payout. This calculator shows the full-year figure for the salary you enter.

Can my employer pay the allowance monthly instead of once a year?

Yes — some employers pay one twelfth of the allowance with each monthly salary instead of a single annual payment, where the contract or collective agreement (CAO) allows it. The annual amount and the tax treatment are the same; only the timing changes, and the special-rate withholding is spread across the year instead of landing in one month.

Is the holiday allowance pensionable salary?

That depends on your pension scheme's rules, not on tax law: many schemes calculate contributions on salary including the allowance, others exclude it. The tax figures in this calculator do not depend on the answer — check your scheme's rules or ask your HR department.

I have two jobs — do I get holiday allowance from both?

Yes. The 8% entitlement applies per employment, on that job's salary, and each employer withholds tax on its own payments. Because the special rate is applied per employer, the annual tax return reconciles the combined total.

Is the allowance also paid over overtime and bonuses?

The statutory minimum is 8% of your wage, but whether overtime, bonuses or commissions sit inside your employer's allowance base depends on your contract or CAO — many employers count fixed salary components only. Enter the salary figure your employer actually applies the 8% to.
